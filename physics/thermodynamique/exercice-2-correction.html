Written correction — Exercise 2

Convention and useful relations

We adopt the convention of quantities received by the gas: $\Delta U = W + Q$, $\delta W = -P\,dV$. For one mole of ideal gas, $PV = RT$ and $\Delta U = C_V(T_f - T_i)$. Mayer's relation gives $C_p - C_V = R$, and writing $\gamma = C_p / C_V$, we have $C_V = R/(\gamma - 1)$ and $C_p = \gamma R / (\gamma - 1)$.

Successive states

In state $A = (V_A, P_A, T_A)$, $T_A = P_A V_A / R$.

$A \to B$ isochoric with $P_B = 2P_A$: $V_B = V_A$, $T_B = 2T_A$. So $B = (V_A,\,2P_A)$.

$B \to C$ isobaric with $V_C = 2V_A$: $P_C = 2P_A$, $T_C = 4T_A$. So $C = (2V_A,\,2P_A)$.

$C \to D$ isothermal with $V_D = 3V_A$: $T_D = T_C = 4T_A$. Since $P_D V_D = R T_D = 4R T_A$:

$$\boxed{P_D = \frac{4}{3}P_A},\qquad D = \left(3V_A,\,\frac{4}{3}P_A\right).$$

Question 1 — Pressure $P_E$

$D \to E$ is a reversible adiabatic transformation: $PV^\gamma = \text{const}$, so $P_E = P_D\,(V_D/V_E)^\gamma$. The statement gives $V_E = (4/3)^{1/\gamma}\,V_D$, so:

$$\frac{V_D}{V_E} = \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{-1/\gamma},\qquad \left(\frac{V_D}{V_E}\right)^\gamma = \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{-1} = \frac{3}{4}.$$

Consequently $P_E = (4/3)P_A \cdot 3/4$, that is:

$$\boxed{P_E = P_A}.$$

The corresponding temperature is $T_E = P_A V_E / R = 3\,(4/3)^{1/\gamma}\,T_A$.

Clapeyron diagram

Visually: $A \to B$ vertical (constant V), $B \to C$ horizontal to the right (constant P), $C \to D$ decreasing hyperbola (isotherm), $D \to E$ a curve steeper than the isotherm (adiabat). Point $E$ lands back on the same horizontal line as $A$, since $P_E = P_A$.

Question 3 — Balance, transformation by transformation

$A \to B$ — isochoric

$$W_{AB} = 0,\qquad \Delta U_{AB} = C_V(T_B - T_A) = C_V T_A,\qquad Q_{AB} = \Delta U_{AB} = C_V T_A.$$

The piston does not move; all the heat received goes into increasing the internal energy.

$B \to C$ — isobaric

$W_{BC} = -P_B(V_C - V_B) = -2P_A V_A = -2RT_A$ (expansion, the gas delivers work).

$\Delta U_{BC} = C_V(T_C - T_B) = 2C_V T_A$.

$Q_{BC} = \Delta U_{BC} - W_{BC} = 2C_V T_A + 2RT_A = 2(C_V + R)T_A = 2C_p T_A$.

$C \to D$ — isothermal

$T = 4T_A$, so $P(V) = 4RT_A / V$. Work:

$$W_{CD} = -\!\int_{V_C}^{V_D}\!\frac{4RT_A}{V}\,dV = -4RT_A\,\ln\!\frac{3V_A}{2V_A} = -4RT_A\,\ln\!\frac{3}{2}.$$

$\Delta U_{CD} = 0$ (constant T), so $Q_{CD} = -W_{CD} = 4RT_A\,\ln(3/2)$.

$D \to E$ — reversible adiabatic

$Q_{DE} = 0$ by definition. And $\Delta U_{DE} = W_{DE} = C_V(T_E - T_D) = C_V T_A\!\left[3(4/3)^{1/\gamma} - 4\right]$.

Total change in internal energy

$U$ is a state function:

$$\boxed{\Delta U_{ABCDE} = C_V(T_E - T_A) = C_V T_A\!\left[3\!\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{\!1/\gamma}\!-\,1\right]}.$$

We can check by summing $\Delta U$ step by step: $C_V T_A + 2C_V T_A + 0 + C_V T_A[3(4/3)^{1/\gamma} - 4] = C_V T_A[3(4/3)^{1/\gamma} - 1]$.

Question 4 — Direct transformation AE

Since $P_E = P_A$, the direct path $A \to E$ is an isobaric transformation at pressure $P_A$. Therefore:

$$W_{AE} = -P_A(V_E - V_A) = -R(T_E - T_A),\qquad \Delta U_{AE} = C_V(T_E - T_A),\qquad Q_{AE} = C_p(T_E - T_A).$$

Question 5 — Graphical comparison

On the diagram, the path $ABCDE$ runs above the path $AE$ (states $B$, $C$, $D$ are at pressures higher than $P_A$). The area under $ABCDE$ is therefore larger than the area under $AE$. Since the work received is the opposite of the area (positive for a compression, negative for an expansion), and both paths are overall expansions:

$$\boxed{W_{ABCDE} < W_{AE} < 0}.$$

The gas delivers more work to the surroundings by taking the detour $ABCDE$.

By contrast, the internal energy depends only on the initial and final states:

$$\boxed{\Delta U_{ABCDE} = \Delta U_{AE} = C_V(T_E - T_A)}.$$

This is the very signature of a state function, as opposed to $W$ and $Q$, which depend on the path. This distinction is central in thermodynamics.
