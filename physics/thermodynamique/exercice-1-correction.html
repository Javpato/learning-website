Written correction — Exercise 1

The full text, to read at your own pace. To visualize the three paths and their areas, open the animated version.

1. Analysis of states A, B and C

The gas is assumed to be ideal, so it satisfies the equation of state $PV = nRT$. With $n = 2$, we have $PV = 2RT$.

In state $A$: $P_A V_A = 2RT_A$. In state $B$: $P_B V_B = 2RT_B$. The statement gives $T_B = T_A$, so $P_B V_B = 2RT_A$, and therefore:

$$P_A V_A = P_B V_B.$$

Since $P_B = 3P_A$, we get $P_A V_A = 3 P_A V_B$, that is, after simplification:

$$\boxed{V_B = \frac{V_A}{3}}.$$

The thermodynamic coordinates of the two states are therefore $A = (V_A, P_A)$ and $B = \left(\dfrac{V_A}{3},\,3P_A\right)$.

Position of point C

The path $ACB$ is an isochoric step $A \to C$ followed by an isobaric step $C \to B$. So $V_C = V_A$ (constant volume) and $P_C = P_B = 3P_A$ (constant pressure). Thus:

$$\boxed{C = (V_A,\,3P_A)}.$$

2. Sign convention and first law

The statement asks for the work and heat received by the gas. With this convention:

$$\Delta U = W + Q,\qquad \delta W = -P\,dV,\qquad W = -\!\int_{V_i}^{V_f}\! P\,dV.$$

A compression ($dV < 0$) gives $W > 0$: the gas receives work. An expansion ($dV > 0$) gives $W < 0$. Here, $V_B = V_A/3 < V_A$ — each path is overall a compression, so we expect $W > 0$ for all three paths.

Why ΔU = 0

For an ideal gas, the internal energy depends only on temperature: $\Delta U = nC_V(T_B - T_A)$. Since $T_B = T_A$, we have:

$$\Delta U_{A\to B} = 0.$$

This conclusion holds for all three paths, because $U$ is a state function: only the initial and final temperatures matter. By the first law of thermodynamics, $W + Q = 0$ for each path, so $\boxed{Q = -W}$.

Question 1 — Equation of the isotherm AMB

The transformation $AMB$ is isothermal ($T = T_A$). For an ideal gas, $PV = nRT$, which here gives:

$$\boxed{PV = 2RT_A},\qquad \text{i.e.}\qquad P(V) = \frac{2RT_A}{V}.$$

This is a decreasing rectangular hyperbola in the $(P, V)$ diagram. We check that $A$ and $B$ lie on it: for $A$, $P_A V_A = 2RT_A$; for $B$, $P_B V_B = 3P_A \cdot V_A/3 = P_A V_A = 2RT_A$.

Path AMB — isothermal transformation

Work received

Along the isotherm, $P(V) = 2RT_A / V$, so:

$$W_{AMB} = -\!\int_{V_A}^{V_B}\!\frac{2RT_A}{V}\,dV = -2RT_A\,\bigl[\ln V\bigr]_{V_A}^{V_B} = -2RT_A\,\ln\!\frac{V_B}{V_A}.$$

Since $V_B/V_A = 1/3$ and $\ln(1/3) = -\ln 3$:

$$\boxed{W_{AMB} = 2RT_A\,\ln 3 \approx 5.49\ \text{kJ}}.$$

Heat received

With $\Delta U = 0$, we have $Q_{AMB} = -W_{AMB}$:

$$\boxed{Q_{AMB} = -2RT_A\,\ln 3 \approx -5.49\ \text{kJ}}.$$

The heat received is negative: during this isothermal compression, the gas receives work and gives up exactly the same amount of energy as heat to the thermostat — the internal energy stays constant.

Path ACB — isochoric then isobaric

Step A → C: isochoric

The volume is constant ($V = V_A$, $dV = 0$), so:

$$\boxed{W_{AC} = 0}.$$

The piston does not move — there is no mechanical work done by the pressure forces.

Step C → B: isobaric

The pressure is $P = P_B = 3P_A$ and the volume goes from $V_A$ to $V_A/3$:

$$W_{CB} = -P_B(V_B - V_C) = -3P_A\!\left(\frac{V_A}{3} - V_A\right) = 2P_A V_A.$$

Using $P_A V_A = 2RT_A$:

$$W_{CB} = 4RT_A.$$

Total for ACB: $W_{ACB} = W_{AC} + W_{CB}$, so:

$$\boxed{W_{ACB} = 4RT_A \approx 9.98\ \text{kJ}},\qquad \boxed{Q_{ACB} = -4RT_A \approx -9.98\ \text{kJ}}.$$

Physically, this path imposes a compression at high pressure (along the isobar $C \to B$). The work received is therefore particularly large.

Path ANB — straight line in the (P, V) diagram

Along a straight line joining $A$ to $B$, $P$ varies linearly with $V$. The area under the curve is the area of a trapezoid:

$$\int_{V_A}^{V_B}\! P(V)\,dV = \frac{P_A + P_B}{2}\,(V_B - V_A).$$

Therefore:

$$W_{ANB} = -\frac{P_A + 3P_A}{2}\!\left(\frac{V_A}{3} - V_A\right) = -2P_A\!\left(-\frac{2V_A}{3}\right) = \frac{4}{3}P_A V_A = \frac{8}{3}RT_A.$$ $$\boxed{W_{ANB} = \frac{8}{3}RT_A \approx 6.66\ \text{kJ}},\qquad \boxed{Q_{ANB} = -\frac{8}{3}RT_A \approx -6.66\ \text{kJ}}.$$

Final comparison

We compare the coefficients of $RT_A$:

$$2\ln 3 \approx 2.20,\qquad \frac{8}{3} \approx 2.67,\qquad 4.$$

Hence the inequality, graphically obvious from the areas:

$$\boxed{W_{AMB} < W_{ANB} < W_{ACB}}.$$

And since $Q = -W$ for each path, the order of the heats is reversed:

$$\boxed{Q_{ACB} < Q_{ANB} < Q_{AMB} < 0}.$$

All three heats are negative: in all three cases, the gas gives up heat to the surroundings. The path that receives the most work (ACB) is also the one that gives up the most heat, which is consistent with $\Delta U = 0$.
