Exercise 4 — Dividers

a) Current divider — putting currents in quadrature

If we want a single-phase AC motor to turn, one solution is to drive two currents in quadrature, i.e. $90^\circ$ out of phase, through two separate windings.

We assume the two windings are identical and can be modeled as series $RL$ circuits. We propose connecting a capacitor in series with one of the two coils.

Let $R$ be the resistance of each winding, $L$ its inductance, $C$ the capacitance of the capacitor, $\omega$ the angular frequency of the sinusoidal steady state, $i_1$ the current in the $RL$-only branch, and $i_2$ the current in the branch containing the capacitor in series with the $RL$ circuit.

  1. Will the current $i_2$ lead or lag $i_1$? Then show that we can write the relation $\dfrac{I_1}{I_2} = -j\,k$, where $k > 0$.
  2. Using the current divider, express as a function of $R$ and $L$ the capacitance $C$ that achieves this relation.
  3. What is then the value of the factor $k$?

b) Voltage divider — supply through a line

A load of impedance $Z_2 = 25\,e^{j\pi/3}\ \Omega$ is supplied through a line with resistance $R = 1\ \Omega$ and reactance $X_L = \omega L = 0.5\ \Omega$. The supply voltage has an RMS value of $U = 240\ \text{V}$.

  1. Use the voltage divider to compute the RMS value of the voltage across the load.
  2. Compute the phase shift of the voltage $u$ relative to the voltage across the load.
