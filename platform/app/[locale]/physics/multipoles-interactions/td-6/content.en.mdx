<Statement>

Two equal dipoles are separated along $x$. Calculate $U_{dd}$ when they
are: parallel to $x$; antiparallel to $x$; parallel to $y$; antiparallel
to $y$.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

Use
$U=k_er^{-3}[\mathbf p_1\cdot\mathbf p_2-3(\mathbf p_1\cdot\mathbf e_x)(\mathbf p_2\cdot\mathbf e_x)]$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

In order: $-2k_ep^2/r^3$, $+2k_ep^2/r^3$,
$+k_ep^2/r^3$, $-k_ep^2/r^3$.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

For the axial dipoles, the first dot product is $\pm p^2$, and the product
of the projections is the same $\pm p^2$, producing the factors $-2$ and
$+2$. For the transverse dipoles, the projections onto $\mathbf e_x$
vanish; only $\mathbf p_1\cdot\mathbf p_2=\pm p^2$ remains.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Deciding the sign from the dipoles' parallelism alone.
- Forgetting the $-3$ factor multiplying the axial projections.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td6-02">

<Statement>

A dipole $\mathbf p_2$ rotates at a fixed position in the field
$\mathbf E_1$ of a first dipole. Find the equilibria, their stability, and
the frequency of small oscillations around the stable equilibrium.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

$U=-p_2E_1\cos\theta$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Expand the cosine near the minimum.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

Alignment with $\mathbf E_1$ is stable, anti-alignment is unstable, and
$\omega=\sqrt{p_2E_1/I}$.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

$dU/d\theta=p_2E_1\sin\theta$ vanishes at $0$ and $\pi$.
$U''(0)=p_2E_1>0$, while $U''(\pi)<0$. Near zero,
$U=U_{\min}+\frac12p_2E_1\theta^2+o(\theta^2)$. The equation
$I\theta''=-dU/d\theta$ becomes $I\theta''+p_2E_1\theta=0$, which gives the
angular frequency.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Declaring both orientations stable because the torque is zero there.
- Forgetting the moment of inertia in the angular frequency.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td6-03">

<Statement>

An ion $q$ approaches an isotropic molecule with polarizability $\alpha$.
Derive the energy and radial force.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

$E=k_e|q|/r^2$ and $U_{\mathrm{ind}}=-\alpha E^2/2$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Define $\mathbf e_r$ toward increasing distances before differentiating.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
U=-\frac12\alpha k_e^2\frac{q^2}{r^4},
\qquad
F_r=-2\alpha k_e^2\frac{q^2}{r^5}.
$$

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

Substituting the ionic field into the induced energy directly produces the
$r^{-4}$ law. Then

$$
F_r=-\frac{d}{dr}\left(-\frac12\alpha k_e^2q^2r^{-4}\right)
=-2\alpha k_e^2q^2r^{-5}.
$$

The result is negative regardless of the sign of $q$: the ion attracts the
dipole that it induces.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Using $U=-pE$ and losing the factor $1/2$.
- Predicting repulsion for a negative ion despite the dependence on $q^2$.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td6-04">

<Statement>

A linear molecule has
$\boldsymbol\alpha=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_\perp,\alpha_\perp,\alpha_\parallel)$.
Its axis makes an angle $\theta$ with a field $E$. Find the energy, torque,
and preferred orientation.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

Project the field parallel and perpendicular to the molecular axis.

</Hint>

<Hint>

The generalized torque is $-dU/d\theta$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
U=-\frac12E^2(\alpha_\perp\sin^2\theta+\alpha_\parallel\cos^2\theta),
$$

$$
\tau_\theta=-\frac12E^2(\alpha_\parallel-\alpha_\perp)\sin2\theta.
$$

If $\alpha_\parallel>\alpha_\perp$, the axis aligns with the field.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

In the principal-axis frame,
$U=-\frac12\mathbf E^\top\boldsymbol\alpha\mathbf E$. The field components
are $E\cos\theta$ along the axis and $E\sin\theta$ transverse to it, which
gives the energy. Its derivative gives the torque. When
$\alpha_\parallel>\alpha_\perp$, the energy is minimized for
$\cos^2\theta=1$, that is, $\theta=0$ or $\pi$.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Forgetting to square the field components.
- Confusing the alignment of an unoriented axis with an oriented permanent
  dipole.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>
