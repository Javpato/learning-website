<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td5-01">

<Statement>

Give the first nonzero far-field moment for: an ion; a separated $+q,-q$
pair; $+q$ at $\pm a\mathbf e_x$ and $-2q$ at the origin; four equal
positive charges at the vertices of a tetrahedron.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

Always start with the total charge.

</Hint>

<Hint>

If it is zero, calculate or infer the dipole from symmetry.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

Monopole; dipole; quadrupole; monopole.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

The ion and the four positive charges have a nonzero total charge:
monopole. The opposite pair is neutral but has a moment directed from the
negative charge toward the positive charge: dipole. The $+q,+q,-2q$
configuration is neutral and invariant under inversion, so its dipole
vanishes; its anisotropy leaves a quadrupole.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Jumping straight to the dipole without testing $Q$.
- Inferring “quadrupole” from the tetrahedral shape alone despite the total
  charge.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td5-02">

<Statement>

$+q$ is placed at $z=\pm a$ and $-2q$ at the origin. Show that
$Q=\mathbf p=0$ and derive the leading potential far from the source.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

Use the exact potential on the axis or the polynomial
$P_2(\cos\theta)$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Inversion symmetry cancels the odd moments.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
V(r,\theta)\sim
\frac1{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2qa^2}{r^3}P_2(\cos\theta),
\qquad P_2(u)=\frac12(3u^2-1).
$$

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

$Q=q+q-2q=0$ and
$\mathbf p=qa\mathbf e_z-q a\mathbf e_z=\mathbf0$. In the traceless
convention used in the lesson, the axial quadrupole term extends angularly
with $P_2$. On the axis, $P_2(1)=1$, and the exact expansion gives
$2k_eqa^2/r^3$, which fixes the stated coefficient.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Keeping a dipole term despite the symmetry $z\mapsto-z$.
- Forgetting to state the quadrupole convention.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td5-03">

<Statement>

For discrete charges and a shift of origin $\mathbf a$, prove
$\mathbf p'=\mathbf p-Q\mathbf a$. When is the dipole invariant?

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

The new coordinates are $\mathbf r_i'=\mathbf r_i-\mathbf a$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$\mathbf p'=\mathbf p-Q\mathbf a$; the dipole is invariant if $Q=0$.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

$$
\mathbf p'=\sum_iq_i\mathbf r_i'
=\sum_iq_i(\mathbf r_i-\mathbf a)
=\mathbf p-\mathbf a\sum_iq_i
=\mathbf p-Q\mathbf a.
$$

The total charge does not depend on the origin. For a neutral distribution,
$Q=0$, so the dipole also becomes invariant; for an ion, the origin must
always be specified.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Writing a plus sign without tracking the direction of the origin shift.
- Claiming invariance without checking neutrality.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td5-04">

<Statement>

A distribution in the plane $z=0$ is invariant under $x\mapsto-x$ and
$y\mapsto-y$. Determine the components of the dipole and traceless
quadrupole that must vanish.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

An odd integrand integrates to zero over a symmetric source.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Examine $x_i$ first, then the products $x_ix_j$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

All dipole components and all off-diagonal quadrupole components vanish.
The diagonal components may remain and satisfy
$Q_{xx}+Q_{yy}+Q_{zz}=0$.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

$p_x$ and $p_y$ change sign under the corresponding reflections;
$p_z=0$ because $z=0$. The terms $xy$, $xz$, and $yz$ are either odd or
zero in the plane, so $Q_{xy}=Q_{xz}=Q_{yz}=0$. The squares $x^2,y^2$ are
even: no symmetry forces the diagonal components to vanish. The definition
$3x_ix_j-r^2\delta_{ij}$ nevertheless imposes zero trace.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Also canceling the diagonal components simply because the dipole is zero.
- Forgetting that tracelessness is a structural convention, not an
  additional symmetry.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>
