# Formulaire (formula sheet) — Electromagnetism and interactions

This page collects the results you need to know how to **use and check**. It
does not replace the assumptions: before applying each formula, identify
the source, symmetry, domain, and vector direction.

<Toc />

## Constants, sources, and geometry

<KeyResults title="Charges and currents">

$$
k_e=\frac1{4\pi\varepsilon_0},\qquad
dq=\lambda\,d\ell=\sigma\,dS=\rho\,dV.
$$

$$
dV_{\rm cyl}=r\,dr\,d\varphi\,dz,\qquad
dV_{\rm sph}=r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\varphi.
$$

For a volume current: $I=\iint\mathbf J\cdot d\mathbf S$.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Universal checks">

- dimensions and sign of the source;
- components forbidden by symmetry;
- behavior at the center, boundary, and infinity;
- matching of $V$, $\mathbf E$, or $\mathbf B$ when the model requires it;
- consistency with $\mathbf E=-\nabla V$ and $\mathbf F=-\nabla U$.

</KeyResults>

## Electric field and superposition

<KeyResults title="Coulomb">

For a point charge:

$$
\mathbf E(\mathbf r)=k_eq\frac{\mathbf r-\mathbf r'}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|^3},
\qquad
\mathbf F=q_{\rm test}\mathbf E.
$$

For a distribution:

$$
\mathbf E(\mathbf r)=k_e\int
\frac{\mathbf r-\mathbf r'}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|^3}\,dq.
$$

Always eliminate components by symmetry before integrating.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Useful geometries">

Ring, on its axis:

$$
E_z(z)=k_e\frac{Qz}{(z^2+R^2)^{3/2}}.
$$

Uniform disk, $z>0$:

$$
E_z=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}
\left(1-\frac z{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}\right).
$$

</KeyResults>

## Gauss's theorem

<KeyResults title="Flux and Gauss's law">

$$
\Phi_E=\iint_S\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf S,\qquad
\oiint_S\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf S
=\frac{Q_{\rm enc}}{\varepsilon_0}.
$$

Gauss's theorem calculates $E$ directly only if symmetry fixes the
direction and makes the magnitude constant on the useful parts of the
surface.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Three canonical results">

$$
E_{\rm fil}(r)=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r},\qquad
E_{\rm plan}=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}.
$$

Uniform sphere of radius $R$:

$$
E_{\rm in}(r)=\frac{\rho r}{3\varepsilon_0},\qquad
E_{\rm out}(r)=k_e\frac Q{r^2}.
$$

</KeyResults>

## Electrostatic potential and energy

<KeyResults title="Potential">

$$
V(B)-V(A)=-\int_A^B\mathbf E\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell,\qquad
\mathbf E=-\nabla V.
$$

$$
V_{\rm point}=k_e\frac qR,\qquad
V(\mathbf r)=k_e\sum_i\frac{q_i}{R_i}
=k_e\int\frac{dq}{R}.
$$

$$
\nabla^2V=-\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0},
\qquad
\nabla^2V=0\quad\text{en région sans charge}.
$$

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Energy and force">

$$
U=qV,\qquad
U_{\rm assemblage}=k_e\sum_{i<j}\frac{q_iq_j}{r_{ij}},
\qquad
\mathbf F=-\nabla U.
$$

Each pair of charges must be counted only once.

</KeyResults>

## Electric dipole

<KeyResults title="Moment, far field, and coupling">

$$
\mathbf p=q\mathbf d
\quad\text{ou}\quad
\mathbf p=\int\mathbf r'\rho(\mathbf r')\,dV'.
$$

$$
V_{\rm dip}=k_e\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r}}{r^2},
$$

$$
\mathbf E_{\rm dip}=
\frac{k_e}{r^3}
\left[3(\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf p\right].
$$

$$
\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf p\times\mathbf E,\qquad
U=-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E,\qquad
\mathbf F=\nabla(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E)
$$

for a fixed dipole in a nonuniform field.

</KeyResults>

## Magnetostatics

<KeyResults title="Biot–Savart">

$$
d\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\frac{I\,d\boldsymbol\ell'\times\widehat{\mathbf R}}{R^2}.
$$

$$
B_{\rm fil}(r)=\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r},\qquad
B_{\rm centre\ spire}=\frac{\mu_0I}{2R},
$$

$$
B_{\rm axe\ spire}(z)=
\frac{\mu_0IR^2}{2(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}.
$$

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Ampère">

$$
\oint_C\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=\mu_0I_{\rm enc}.
$$

$$
B_{\rm solenoide}\approx\mu_0nI,\qquad
B_{\rm tore}(r)=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi r}.
$$

Ampère's theorem is computationally useful only if symmetry lets you take
$B$ outside the integral.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Vector potential and magnetic dipole">

$$
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A,\qquad
\mathbf A\to\mathbf A+\nabla\chi.
$$

$$
\mathbf m=IS\mathbf n,\qquad
\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf m\times\mathbf B,\qquad
U=-\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf B.
$$

$$
\mathbf B_{\rm dip}=
\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}
\left[3(\mathbf m\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf m\right].
$$

</KeyResults>

## Multipole expansion

<KeyResults title="Electric moments">

$$
Q=\int\rho\,dV,\qquad
\mathbf p=\int\mathbf r'\rho\,dV',
$$

$$
Q_{ij}=\int\rho(\mathbf r')
(3x_i'x_j'-r'^2\delta_{ij})\,dV'.
$$

$$
V=k_e\left[
\frac Qr+\frac{\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r}}{r^2}
+\frac1{2r^3}\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}\hat r_i\hat r_j+\cdots
\right].
$$

Valid when $r$ is large compared with the size of the source.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Origin and symmetries">

Under a shift of origin $\mathbf a$:

$$
\mathbf p'=\mathbf p-Q\mathbf a.
$$

The dipole is therefore invariant for a neutral source. Inversion symmetry
cancels the dipole; spherical symmetry leaves only the external monopole.

</KeyResults>

## Molecular interactions

<KeyResults title="Permanent dipoles">

$$
U_{q-p}=k_e\frac{q\,\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r}}{r^2},
$$

$$
U_{dd}=\frac{k_e}{r^3}
\left[
\mathbf p_1\cdot\mathbf p_2
-3(\mathbf p_1\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})
(\mathbf p_2\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})
\right].
$$

Scales: charge–charge $r^{-1}$, charge–dipole $r^{-2}$,
dipole–dipole $r^{-3}$.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Polarizability">

$$
\mathbf p_{\rm ind}=\boldsymbol\alpha\mathbf E,\qquad
U_{\rm ind}=-\frac12\mathbf E^\top\boldsymbol\alpha\mathbf E.
$$

Isotropic molecule in the field of an ion:

$$
U=-\frac12\alpha k_e^2\frac{q^2}{r^4},\qquad
F_r=-2\alpha k_e^2\frac{q^2}{r^5}.
$$

The factor $1/2$ is specific to the construction of an induced dipole.

</KeyResults>

## Charged particles

<KeyResults title="Lorentz force and cyclotron">

$$
\mathbf F=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B).
$$

For $\mathbf v_\perp\mathbf B$:

$$
R=\frac{mv_\perp}{|q|B},\qquad
\omega_c=\frac{|q|B}{m},\qquad
\text{pas de l'hélice}=\frac{2\pi mv_\parallel}{|q|B}.
$$

The magnetic force does no work:
$\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)=0$.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Selection and time of flight">

$$
v_{\rm selecteur}=\frac EB,\qquad
R_{\rm analyseur}=\frac{mE}{|q|B_1B_2}.
$$

After acceleration through $\Delta V$:

$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{2|q|\Delta V}{m}},\qquad
t=L\sqrt{\frac{m}{2|q|\Delta V}}.
$$

</KeyResults>

## Optional bridge to Maxwell

<KeyResults title="Induction and local equations">

$$
\mathcal E=-\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt},
$$

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0},\quad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,
$$

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf B,\quad
\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J+
\mu_0\varepsilon_0\partial_t\mathbf E.
$$

In vacuum, $c=(\mu_0\varepsilon_0)^{-1/2}$. This block is an enrichment,
not content confirmed as central to L2 Chimie.

</KeyResults>

## Exam strategy

1. Draw the source, observation point, and vectors.
2. Use symmetries before choosing Coulomb, Gauss, Biot–Savart, or Ampère.
3. State the assumptions behind the dipole or multipole approximation.
4. Finish by checking dimensions, direction, sign, and limit.
