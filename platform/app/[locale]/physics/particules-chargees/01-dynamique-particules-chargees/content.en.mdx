## Intuition

Release a $q$ charge into an electric field: it is pushed *along* of $\mathbf E$, like a mass in a gravitational field. It gains speed, therefore kinetic energy.

Now launch the same charge into a uniform magnetic field. The magnetic force $q\,\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ is **always perpendicular to the velocity**: it can neither accelerate nor slow down the particle, only *bend* its trajectory. Perpendicular and of constant magnitude — that is exactly the recipe for uniform circular motion. The magnetic field is a flywheel, not an engine.

Overlay the two effects and you get the entire toolbox: an (electric) accelerator, a calibrated (magnetic) turn, and — crossed fields — a filter that lets through only a single speed.

<KeyResults title="Formulas to keep visible">

- Lorentz: $\mathbf F=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$.
- Cyclotron: $R=mv_\perp/(|q|B)$ and $\omega_c=|q|B/m$.
- Speed selector: $v=E/B$; a magnetic field alone does not change kinetic energy.

</KeyResults>

## The Lorentz force

$$
\mathbf F = q\,(\mathbf E + \mathbf v\times\mathbf B).
$$

- $q$: particle charge, **with its sign** (in C);
- $\mathbf E$: electric field (V·m$^{-1}$);
- $\mathbf B$: magnetic field (T);
- $\mathbf v$: particle velocity (m·s$^{-1}$).

Assumptions of the framework: mechanics **non-relativistic** ($v \ll c$), *given* external fields (the particle does not modify its sources), and radiation from the accelerated charge neglected. With the fundamental principle of dynamics:

$$
m\,\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt} = q\,(\mathbf E + \mathbf v\times\mathbf B).
$$

### Radius and cyclotron angular frequency

For $\mathbf E = \mathbf 0$ and $\mathbf v \perp \mathbf B$, the trajectory is a **circle** traveled at a constant speed:

$$
R = \frac{m v}{|q| B},
\qquad
\omega_c = \frac{|q| B}{m}.
$$

- $R$ : cyclotron radius (m) — the **heavy or fast** particles follow wider trajectories;
- $\omega_c$: cyclotron angular frequency (rad·s$^{-1}$) — remarkable: it does **not** depend on the speed, only on the ratio $|q|/m$.

The direction of rotation depends on the **sign** of $q$: a positive charge and a negative charge rotate in opposite directions in the same field.

### Helical motion

If $\mathbf v$ also has a $v_\parallel$ component along $\mathbf B$, it is not affected ($\mathbf v_\parallel \times \mathbf B = \mathbf 0$): the particle drifts uniformly along the field lines while the perpendicular component rotates. The trajectory is a **helix** with an axis $\mathbf B$, a radius $R = m v_\perp/(|q|B)$, and a **pitch**

$$
p = v_\parallel\, T = \frac{2\pi m\, v_\parallel}{|q| B},
$$

the distance advanced along the axis during a complete turn ($T = 2\pi/\omega_c$).

### The magnetic force never does work

$$
P_{\text{mag}} = \mathbf F_{\text{mag}}\cdot\mathbf v
= q\,(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\cdot\mathbf v = 0 .
$$

The mixed product is zero because $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf v$. Consequence: in a purely magnetic field, $\|\mathbf v\|$ and kinetic energy are **constant**. Only the electric field changes the energy:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\tfrac12 m v^2\right) = q\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v .
$$

The complete demonstration can be expanded below (and it is exercise P7.3 of TD).

### Speed selector (crossed fields)

With $\mathbf E \perp \mathbf B$, both perpendicular to the incident velocity, the two forces oppose each other. They exactly cancel each other out for

$$
v = \frac{E}{B}.
$$

A particle at this speed flies **in a straight line** — and this criterion depends on neither $m$ nor $q$ (nor even a sign of the charge: the two forces change sign together). Any faster or slower particle is deflected and eliminated by a slit. This is the entry stage of the Bainbridge spectrometer.

</Collapsible>

## Worked example — separating neon isotopes

A Bainbridge spectrometer sorts $^{20}\text{Ne}^+$ ions and
$^{22}\text{Ne}^+$ (charge $+e$). Selector: $E = 1{,}0\times10^5$
V·m$^{-1}$, $B_1 = 0{,}50$ T. Analyzer: field $B_2 = 0{,}50$ T, perpendicular to the velocity.

**Step 1 — selected speed.**

$$
v = \frac{E}{B_1} = \frac{1{,}0\times10^5}{0{,}50}
= 2{,}0\times10^5\ \text{m·s}^{-1},
$$

identical for both isotopes: that is the whole point of the selector.

**Step 2 — masses.** With $1\ \text{u} = 1{,}66\times10^{-27}$ kg: $m_{20} \approx 3{,}32\times10^{-26}$ kg and $m_{22} \approx 3{,}65\times10^{-26}$ kg.

**Step 3 — beams in the analyzer.**

$$
R_{20} = \frac{m_{20}\,v}{e\,B_2}
= \frac{3{,}32\times10^{-26}\times 2{,}0\times10^5}
{1{,}60\times10^{-19}\times 0{,}50}
\approx 8{,}3\ \text{cm},
\qquad
R_{22} \approx 9{,}1\ \text{cm}.
$$

**Step 4 — separation on the detector.** The impacts are separated by $2(R_{22}-R_{20}) \approx 1{,}7$ cm: easily measurable. This is how Aston demonstrated the existence of stable isotopes — and the $R_{22}/R_{20} = 22/20$ ratio directly gives back the mass ratio.

<Collapsible title="Demonstration — the cyclotron circle from Newton">

Let's take $\mathbf B = B\,\mathbf e_z$, $\mathbf E = \mathbf 0$ and an initial velocity in the $xy$ plane. The equations of motion $m\dot{\mathbf v} = q\,\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ are written in components:

$$
m\dot v_x = q B\, v_y,
\qquad
m\dot v_y = -q B\, v_x,
\qquad
m\dot v_z = 0 .
$$

Let's set $\omega = qB/m$ and derive the first equation:

$$
\ddot v_x = \omega\,\dot v_y = -\omega^2 v_x .
$$

This is the equation of a harmonic oscillator: $v_x$ and $v_y$ oscillate at the angular frequency $|\omega| = \omega_c = |q|B/m$, in quadrature, with $v_x^2 + v_y^2 = v_\perp^2$ constant. The trajectory, integral of a rotating velocity of constant magnitude, is a uniformly traversed circle. Its radius links velocity and angular frequency:

$$
v_\perp = \omega_c R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R = \frac{v_\perp}{\omega_c} = \frac{m v_\perp}{|q| B}.
$$

It can be found without solving: the magnetic, centripetal force is $|q| v_\perp B = m v_\perp^2/R$, hence the same $R$. The sign of $\omega = qB/m$ determines the direction of rotation: it changes with the sign of $q$.
$\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="Demonstration — magnetic force does not do work">

Let's start from the theorem of kinetic energy in its instantaneous form. By taking the scalar product of the equation of motion with $\mathbf v$:

$$
m\,\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt}\cdot\mathbf v
= q\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v
+ q\,(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\cdot\mathbf v .
$$

The left-hand side is an exact derivative:

$$
m\,\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt}\cdot\mathbf v
= \frac{d}{dt}\left(\tfrac12 m\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v\right)
= \frac{d}{dt}\left(\tfrac12 m v^2\right).
$$

The last term is a mixed product with two identical vectors: $(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\cdot\mathbf v = 0$, because $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ is by construction orthogonal to $\mathbf v$. It remains

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\tfrac12 m v^2\right) = q\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v :
$$

only the electric field exchanges energy with the particle. In a purely magnetic field, $v = \|\mathbf v\|$ is a constant of motion — the trajectory can be twisted as much as you want, the energy meter does not move. $\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

## Connection to chemistry

Mass spectrometry is *the direct application* of this lesson. A magnetic sector spectrometer exactly links our three building blocks:

1. **ionization then acceleration** under tension $\Delta V$ — the electric field does work: $\tfrac12 m v^2 = q\,\Delta V$ ;
2. **speed selection** by crossed fields: only the ions at $v = E/B_1$ continue;
3. **magnetic analysis**: the radius $R = mv/(|q|B_2)$ sorts ions according to $m/|q|$ — the famous $m/z$ of spectra.

Two chemically almost identical isotopes ($^{35}$Cl and $^{37}$Cl, $^{12}$C and $^{13}$C…) land at different rays: the mass spectrum *counts the nucleons*. The resulting isotopic clusters indicate the elemental composition of a molecule. And the "time-of-flight" (TOF) variant, where accelerated ions travel over a length $L$ in a time $t \propto \sqrt{m}$, is the subject of exercise P7.4.

## Classic traps

<Pitfall>

**Believe that the magnetic field accelerates the particle.** It curves the trajectory but never changes $\|\mathbf v\|$: the power $q(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\cdot\mathbf v$ is identically zero. If a calculation gives you a kinetic energy modified only by $\mathbf B$, it is wrong.

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**Forget the sign of the charge.** In $\mathbf F = q\,\mathbf v\times \mathbf B$, an electron ($q = -e$) is deflected in the *opposite* direction to a proton and rotates in the reverse direction. The formulas of $R$ and $\omega_c$ contain $|q|$; the **direction** of rotation, meanwhile, comes from the sign.

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**Use $v$ total instead of $v_\perp$ in the radius.** For a helix, only the component perpendicular to $\mathbf B$ rotates: $R = m v_\perp/(|q|B)$, while $v_\parallel$ fixes the pitch. Mixing the two distorts radius *and* pitch.

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**Confusing the roles of $E$ and $B$ in the selector.** The condition $v = E/B$ comes from the balance $|q|E = |q|vB$: it is a field ratio, not a property of the particle. It depends neither on $m$ nor on $q$ — this is precisely why the selector sorts *the speed* and nothing else.

</Pitfall>

## Checks

**Dimensions.** One tesla is $1\ \text{T} = 1\ \text{kg·s}^{-2} \text{·A}^{-1}$, so $|q|B$ is measured in $\text{C}\cdot\text{kg·s}^{-2}\text{·A}^{-1} = \text{kg·s}^{-1}$. So:

- $R = mv/(|q|B)$ : $(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m·s}^{-1})/(\text{kg·s}^{-1}) = \text{m}$ ✓ ;
- $\omega_c = |q|B/m$ : $\text{kg·s}^{-1}/\text{kg} = \text{s}^{-1}$ ✓ ;
- $E/B$ : $(\text{V·m}^{-1})/\text{T} = \text{m·s}^{-1}$ ✓.

**Limiting cases.**

- $B \to 0$ : $R \to \infty$ — the trajectory tends towards a straight line, consistent with the absence of force.
- $m$ large at $v$ set: $R$ large, $\omega_c$ small — heavy ions turn wide and slowly, exactly what the spectrometer sorts.
- $v_\parallel = 0$: zero pitch: the helix becomes a circle; $v_\perp = 0$: straight line along $\mathbf B$.

## Lesson summary

<KeyResults title="The essentials">

- Lorentz force: $\mathbf F = q(\mathbf E + \mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$ — with the **sign** of $q$.
- Uniform field $\mathbf B$, $\mathbf v\perp\mathbf B$: circle of radius $R = mv/(|q|B)$, pulsation $\omega_c = |q|B/m$ (independent of $v$).
-  Component $v_\parallel$ in addition: helix pitch $2\pi m v_\parallel/(|q|B)$.
- The magnetic force **never works**: $\frac{d}{dt}(\tfrac12 mv^2) = q\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v$.
- Crossed-field selector: straight-through mode for $v = E/B$, independently of $m$ and $q$.
-  Mass spectrometry = acceleration ($qV$) + selection ($E/B$) + magnetic analysis ($R \propto m/|q|$): the physics of this lesson, in instrument.

</KeyResults>
