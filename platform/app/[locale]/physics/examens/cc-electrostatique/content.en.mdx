<ExamProblem title="Question 1 — Infinite charged plane" points={4}>

An infinite plane carries a uniform surface charge density $\sigma$. Use
its symmetries to determine the form of the field, then calculate its
magnitude with Gauss's theorem.

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème (mark scheme)">

**Symmetries (2 pts).** Translations in the plane make the magnitude
independent of the parallel position; rotations around the normal rule out
any tangential component. Reflection exchanges the two sides and reverses
the field: $\mathbf E=E\,\mathbf n$ on one side and
$-E\,\mathbf n$ on the other.

**Gauss (2 pts).** A pillbox of area $S$ gives
$2ES=\sigma S/\varepsilon_0$, so

$$
E=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}.
$$

</Collapsible>

</ExamProblem>

<ExamProblem title="Question 2 — From potential to sources" points={5}>

You are given $V(x,z)=V_0e^{-az}\cos(bx)$. Calculate $\mathbf E$, then the
charge density $\rho$ implied by Poisson's equation.

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème">

**Gradient (3 pts).**

$$
\mathbf E=-\nabla V
=V_0e^{-az}
\left(b\sin(bx)\mathbf e_x+a\cos(bx)\mathbf e_z\right).
$$

**Poisson (2 pts).** Since $\nabla^2V=(a^2-b^2)V$ and
$\nabla^2V=-\rho/\varepsilon_0$,

$$
\rho=-\varepsilon_0(a^2-b^2)V.
$$

</Collapsible>

</ExamProblem>

<ExamProblem title="Question 3 — Charged ring" points={5}>

A ring of radius $R$ and total charge $Q$ lies in the plane $z=0$.
Establish its field on the axis and verify the far-field limit.

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème">

By symmetry, only the axial components add. Every element is at distance
$\sqrt{R^2+z^2}$, and the axial projection is
$z/\sqrt{R^2+z^2}$:

$$
E_z=\frac1{4\pi\varepsilon_0}
\frac{Qz}{(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}.
$$

For $|z|\gg R$, $E_z\sim k_eQz/|z|^3$, the field of a point charge $Q$.
Barème: symmetry 1 pt, integrand/projection 2 pts, result 1 pt, limit
1 pt.

</Collapsible>

</ExamProblem>

<ExamProblem title="Question 4 — Radially nonuniform current" points={6}>

An infinite conducting cylinder of radius $R$ carries
$J(r)=Cr$ along its axis and a total current $I$. Find $C$, then $B(r)$
inside and outside.

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème">

$$
I=\int_0^RCr\,2\pi r\,dr=\frac{2\pi CR^3}{3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C=\frac{3I}{2\pi R^3}.
$$

For $r<R$, $I_{\rm enc}=2\pi Cr^3/3=I(r/R)^3$. Ampère's theorem gives

$$
B_{\rm in}(r)=\frac{\mu_0Ir^2}{2\pi R^3},
\qquad
B_{\rm out}(r)=\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}.
$$

The field is azimuthal and continuous at $R$. Barème: normalization 2 pts,
symmetry and enclosed current 2 pts, two regions and check 2 pts.

</Collapsible>

</ExamProblem>
