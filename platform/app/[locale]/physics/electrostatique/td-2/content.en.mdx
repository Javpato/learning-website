<Statement>

A sphere of radius $R$ has $\rho(r)=\rho_0r/R$. Find the total charge,
$\mathbf E$ inside and outside, and verify continuity at $R$.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

$Q_{\mathrm{enc}}(r)=\int_0^r\rho(s)4\pi s^2ds$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

On a Gaussian sphere,
$E\,4\pi r^2=Q_{\mathrm{enc}}/\varepsilon_0$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
Q=\pi\rho_0R^3,\quad
E_{\rm in}=\frac{\rho_0r^2}{4\varepsilon_0R},\quad
E_{\rm out}=\frac{\rho_0R^3}{4\varepsilon_0r^2}.
$$

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

$$
Q_{\mathrm{enc}}(r)=\frac{4\pi\rho_0}{R}\int_0^rs^3ds
=\frac{\pi\rho_0r^4}{R}.
$$

Gauss's theorem gives the inner expression. At $r=R$, it gives
$Q=\pi\rho_0R^3$, followed by the outer field of this total charge. Both
expressions equal $\rho_0R/(4\varepsilon_0)$ at the boundary: they are
continuous, consistently with the absence of a singular surface charge.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Replacing the enclosed charge by the total charge inside the sphere.
- Forgetting the $s^2$ factor in the spherical volume element.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td2-02">

<Statement>

With $V(\infty)=0$, calculate the potential of the sphere from the previous
exercise, inside and outside.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

Outside, integrate the field proportional to $1/r^2$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Inside, impose continuity of $V$ at $R$, then integrate from $R$ to $r$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
V_{\rm out}=\frac{\rho_0R^3}{4\varepsilon_0r},
\qquad
V_{\rm in}=\frac{\rho_0R^2}{4\varepsilon_0}
+\frac{\rho_0(R^3-r^3)}{12\varepsilon_0R}.
$$

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

The outer field gives $V_{\rm out}=Q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r)$, which is the
first expression. Next,

$$
V(r)=V(R)+\int_r^R E_{\rm in}(s)\,ds
=\frac{\rho_0R^2}{4\varepsilon_0}
+\frac{\rho_0}{4\varepsilon_0R}\frac{R^3-r^3}{3}.
$$

$V$ and its radial derivative match at the boundary.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Imposing $V(R)=0$ instead of using the reference at infinity.
- Losing the sign in $V(r)-V(R)=-\int_R^rE\,ds$.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td2-03">

<Statement>

An infinite solid cylinder $r<a$ carries a density $\rho$. A shell at
$r=b>a$ makes the total charge per unit length zero. Find its surface
charge density, the field in the three regions, and $V(r)-V(a)$ for
$a<r<b$.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

Neutrality per unit length compares $\rho\pi a^2$ with $\sigma2\pi b$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Use a Gaussian cylinder of length $L$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
\sigma=-\frac{\rho a^2}{2b};
\quad E=\frac{\rho r}{2\varepsilon_0}\ (r<a),\
E=\frac{\rho a^2}{2\varepsilon_0r}\ (a<r<b),\
E=0\ (r>b),
$$

$$
V(r)-V(a)=-\frac{\rho a^2}{2\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac ra.
$$

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

Neutrality gives $\rho\pi a^2+\sigma2\pi b=0$. In a Gaussian cylinder,
$E2\pi rL=Q_{\rm enc}/\varepsilon_0$. The enclosed charge is
$\rho\pi r^2L$ in the solid cylinder, $\rho\pi a^2L$ between the two
surfaces, and then zero beyond the shell. Finally, in the gap,

$$
V(r)-V(a)=-\int_a^r\frac{\rho a^2}{2\varepsilon_0s}\,ds.
$$

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Comparing a volume charge with a surface charge without reasoning per
  unit length.
- Including the shell in a Gaussian surface located before $b$.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td2-04">

<Statement>

Four charges alternate $+q,-q,+q,-q$ around a square of side $a$.
Calculate the total assembly energy.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

Count each pair only once.

</Hint>

<Hint>

The four edges connect opposite signs; the two diagonals connect identical
signs.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
U=\frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0a}(-4+\sqrt2).
$$

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

Each edge contributes $-k_eq^2/a$, for a total of $-4k_eq^2/a$. Each
diagonal of length $a\sqrt2$ contributes $+k_eq^2/(a\sqrt2)$; together
they give $+\sqrt2\,k_eq^2/a$. Their sum is the stated expression, which is
negative: the alternating configuration is bound relative to infinitely
separated charges.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Counting the ordered pairs $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$ and thus doubling the
  energy.
- Taking the diagonal to be $2a$.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>
