<Statement>

The charges $+q$ and $-q$ are at $z=\pm a$. Write the exact potential in
spherical coordinates, expand it for $r\gg a$, and identify the dipole.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

The distances are $\sqrt{r^2+a^2\mp2ar\cos\theta}$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Expand $(1+u)^{-1/2}$ to first order.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
V=k_eq\left[
(r^2+a^2-2ar\cos\theta)^{-1/2}
-(r^2+a^2+2ar\cos\theta)^{-1/2}
\right]
\sim k_e\frac{2qa\cos\theta}{r^2},
$$

so $\mathbf p=2qa\,\mathbf e_z$.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

The potential is the scalar sum of the two signed contributions. Far from
the source, the $1/r$ terms cancel and the corrections linear in $a/r$ add,
giving $2k_eqa\cos\theta/r^2$. It agrees with
$k_e(\mathbf p\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r})/r^2$. The source's odd parity then
leaves the octupole; the quadrupole vanishes.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Subtracting field vectors instead of adding scalar potentials.
- Identifying $p=qa$ even though the separation between the charges is
  $2a$.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td3-02">

<Statement>

Model water using two charges $+q$ on the H atoms, each at distance $\ell$
from O and separated by the angle $\theta$, and a charge $-2q$ on O.
Calculate the molecular dipole moment.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

Choose the origin at O: its contribution to the dipole is zero.

</Hint>

<Hint>

The components perpendicular to the angle bisector cancel.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

The dipole lies along the angle bisector, toward the H atoms, and
$p=2q\ell\cos(\theta/2)$.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

Each bond contributes a vector of magnitude $q\ell$. By symmetry, the
transverse components are opposite, while the components along the
bisector are each $q\ell\cos(\theta/2)$. Their sum gives the result. For a
numerical value in debyes, use
$1\ \mathrm D=3{,}33564\times10^{-30}\ \mathrm{C\,m}$.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Adding the magnitudes and obtaining $2q\ell$ independently of the angle.
- Adding a moment from the charge on O despite choosing the origin at O.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td3-03">

<Statement>

A dipole of magnitude $p$ and moment of inertia $I$ rotates in a uniform
field of magnitude $E$. Establish its equation and the frequency of small
oscillations around the stable equilibrium.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

The projected torque is $-pE\sin\theta$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

Near zero, $\sin\theta\approx\theta$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$I\theta''=-pE\sin\theta$ and $\omega=\sqrt{pE/I}$.

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

The energy is $U=-pE\cos\theta$, so
$\tau_\theta=-dU/d\theta=-pE\sin\theta$. Rotational dynamics gives
$I\theta''=\tau_\theta$. Around $\theta=0$, the equation becomes
$I\theta''+pE\theta=0$, the equation of a harmonic oscillator with angular
frequency $\sqrt{pE/I}$. The equilibrium at $\theta=\pi$ is unstable.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Linearizing around $\pi$ and keeping the wrong sign.
- Confusing torque with translational force.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="phys-em-td3-04">

<Statement>

A fixed dipole $\mathbf p=p\mathbf e_z$ is placed on the positive axis, at
distance $z$ from a charge $Q$ at the origin. Find its energy and axial
force.

</Statement>

<HintLadder>

<Hint>

On the axis, $\mathbf E_Q=k_eQ\,\mathbf e_z/z^2$.

</Hint>

<Hint>

$U=-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E$, then $F_z=-dU/dz$.

</Hint>

</HintLadder>

<FinalAnswer>

$$
U=-\frac{k_eQp}{z^2},\qquad
F_z=-\frac{2k_eQp}{z^3}.
$$

</FinalAnswer>

<Solution>

The imposed alignment directly gives $U=-pE_Q=-k_eQp/z^2$. Taking
$\mathbf e_z$ to point toward increasing $z$,

$$
F_z=-\frac{dU}{dz}
=-\frac{d}{dz}(-k_eQpz^{-2})
=-\frac{2k_eQp}{z^3}.
$$

For $Qp>0$, the negative sign indicates attraction toward the charge.

</Solution>

<CommonErrors>

- Using $U=qV$, the formula for a point charge, for a dipole.
- Losing the outer sign in $\mathbf F=-\nabla U$.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>
