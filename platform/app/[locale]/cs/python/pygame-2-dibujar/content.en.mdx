# Drawing on Screen (Pygame)

<Accroche>« A screen is a grid of pixels. Drawing is saying which color goes where. »</Accroche>

You already have a window. Now let's **paint** on it: rectangles, circles and colors. It all rests on
the **coordinate** system, which in Pygame has an important quirk.

## Coordinates: y grows downward

The origin `(0, 0)` is at the **top left**. `x` grows to the right (normal) and `y` grows **downward**
(the opposite of math!). It's the convention of almost all screens.

```text
(0,0) ────────► x
  │
  │
  ▼ y
```

## Colors and shapes

Colors are tuples `(red, green, blue)`, from 0 to 255. Shapes are drawn onto the screen:

```python
NEGRO = (0, 0, 0)
ROJO = (255, 0, 0)

pantalla.fill(NEGRO)                                  # background
pygame.draw.rect(pantalla, ROJO, (50, 40, 100, 60))   # rect: x, y, width, height
pygame.draw.circle(pantalla, (0, 120, 255), (300, 200), 30)  # center (x,y), radius
pygame.display.flip()
```

## Practice: computing positions

To place things in a row (a level's tiles, for example), you compute their `x` from a fixed width.
That's pure Python:

{/* sol:
ANCHO_TILE = 32
posiciones = [i * ANCHO_TILE for i in range(4)]
print(posiciones)
*/}

<Pitfall>
Remember that `y` grows **downward**: an object at `y = 0` is right at the top, and to move it down you
**add** to `y`. This will be key when jumping (physics chapter): jumping is giving `y` a **negative**
velocity.
</Pitfall>

## Mini-exercises

<Collapsible title="1. What color is (255, 255, 0)?">

Yellow: red maxed out + green maxed out, no blue. Mixing red and green light gives yellow (additive
synthesis, like on screens).

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="2. In what order does drawing happen?">

Whatever you draw **later** covers what came before, like layers of paint. That's why the background
`pantalla.fill(...)` goes **first**, and the player and enemies after.

</Collapsible>
