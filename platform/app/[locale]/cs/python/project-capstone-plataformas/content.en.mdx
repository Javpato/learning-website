# Capstone — Platformer (your own mini-Mario)

This is the **final project** of your journey: a complete **platformer**, Mario-style, with a character
who runs and jumps, platforms, coins, enemies, several levels, sprites and sound. It's ambitious on
purpose — and, like the whole course, you write it **on your machine** and push it to your GitHub. It'll

We don't give it to you solved: we give you the **plan**, the **architecture** and the **key ideas**.
Build it by milestones, pushing a commit as you finish each one.

## What you'll build

A character that moves with the arrows, jumps with real physics (gravity and a parabola), lands on
platforms, collects coins, avoids or defeats enemies, and reaches the goal — across several levels, with
a score and lives.

## Architecture (several files)

A project this size does **not** fit in a single `main.py`. Split it up (Module 4 and chapter 18):

```text
mini-plataformas/
├── main.py         # startup + main game loop
├── settings.py     # constants: ANCHO, ALTO, GRAVEDAD, FPS, colors
├── player.py       # Player class: movement, jump, gravity, collisions, animation
├── level.py        # Level class: platforms, coins, enemies, goal; draws and updates
├── entities.py     # Coin, Enemy, Platform (or dataclasses / Pygame sprites)
├── assets/
│   ├── images/     # character sprites, tiles, coins
│   └── sounds/     # jump, coin, damage
├── requirements.txt
├── README.md
└── capturas/
```

## Build by milestones

Each milestone is a slightly more complete playable game — and a commit.

### Milestone 1 — Window and a square that moves
Pygame game loop (events → update → draw → `tick(FPS)`). A `Player` that moves left and right with the
arrows. No gravity yet.

### Milestone 2 — Gravity and jumping
Give the `Player` a vertical velocity `vy`. Each frame: `vy += GRAVEDAD`, `y += vy`. When you press jump
and are on the ground, `vy = -FUERZA_SALTO`. Now you have the parabola you simulated in chapter 18, on
screen.

### Milestone 3 — Platforms and collisions
A list of platforms (rectangles). When the player falls onto one, stop the fall (`vy = 0`) and rest them
on top. Use `pygame.Rect` and `colliderect` to detect the hits.

### Milestone 4 — Coins and score
Scatter coins around the level. When you touch them (`colliderect`), add points, play them with a sound
and remove them. Show the score on screen.

### Milestone 5 — Enemies and lives
Enemies that patrol back and forth. If the player touches them from the side, they lose a life; if they
land on them (like in Mario), they defeat them. With 0 lives, "game over".

### Milestone 6 — Levels and goal
A flag or door that, when reached, moves to the next level. Store the levels as **data** (a list of
positions, or a text map) that `level.py` reads, not hardcoded in the code.

### Milestone 7 — Polish
Sprites and images instead of rectangles, animation when running/jumping, sounds, start and end
screens, and a camera that follows the player if the level is wider than the screen.

## Key ideas

- **The loop order rules**: move (physics) → resolve collisions → draw. In that order, or the character
  will jitter or go through the ground (chapter 18).
- **y grows downward** on screen: that's why jumping is a **negative** `vy`.
- **Levels are data, not code**: that way you add levels without touching the logic (the same principle
  as the shop catalog or the tic-tac-toe lines).
- **One class per entity** (`Player`, `Enemy`, `Coin`): each one knows how to update and draw itself.
  Pygame even provides `pygame.sprite.Sprite` and `Group` to manage them together.

<Pitfall>
Don't try to do it all at once. If you start with the sprites and enemies before you have a square that
jumps on a platform, you'll get stuck. **One playable milestone at a time**: first it moves, then it
jumps, then it lands… Each step is a commit and a small victory.
</Pitfall>
