# Project 4 · Order report

<Accroche>« A single order touches four tables. Time to weave them into one report. »</Accroche>

## The brief

The finance team wants a proper **order report**: who bought what, how much of it, and at
what price. That information is spread across `customers`, `orders`, `order_items` and
`products`, linked by keys. This project (cumulative over Chapters 1–4) builds the report
one join at a time.

## Tasks

### 1 · Orders with customer names

<SqlExercise
  task="List each order_id with the full name of the customer who placed it (first and last joined by a space)."
  starter={`SELECT o.order_id, c.first_name || ' ' || c.last_name AS customer\nFROM orders o\nJOIN customers c ON `}
  expected="SELECT o.order_id, c.first_name || ' ' || c.last_name AS customer FROM orders o JOIN customers c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id;"
  hint="Join orders to customers on customer_id, then concatenate names with ||."
/>

### 2 · Order lines with product details

<SqlExercise
  task="For every order line, show the order_id, the product name, the quantity, and the unit_price."
  starter={`SELECT oi.order_id, p.name, oi.quantity, oi.unit_price\nFROM order_items oi\nJOIN products p ON `}
  expected="SELECT oi.order_id, p.name, oi.quantity, oi.unit_price FROM order_items oi JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.product_id;"
  hint="Join order_items to products on product_id."
/>

### 3 · Customers who never ordered

<SqlExercise
  task="List the first and last name of every customer who has never placed an order."
  starter={`SELECT c.first_name, c.last_name\nFROM customers c\nLEFT JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id\nWHERE `}
  expected="SELECT c.first_name, c.last_name FROM customers c LEFT JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id WHERE o.order_id IS NULL;"
  hint="LEFT JOIN orders, then keep only rows where o.order_id IS NULL."
/>

### 4 · Products never sold

<SqlExercise
  task="List the names of products that have never appeared in any order."
  starter={`SELECT p.name\nFROM products p\nLEFT JOIN order_items oi ON p.product_id = oi.product_id\nWHERE `}
  expected="SELECT p.name FROM products p LEFT JOIN order_items oi ON p.product_id = oi.product_id WHERE oi.order_id IS NULL;"
  hint="LEFT JOIN order_items, then keep rows where the order_items side is NULL."
/>

### 5 · Line revenue

<SqlExercise
  task="For each order line, show the order_id, the product name, and the line revenue (quantity * unit_price)."
  starter={`SELECT oi.order_id, p.name, oi.quantity * oi.unit_price AS revenue\nFROM order_items oi\nJOIN products p ON `}
  expected="SELECT oi.order_id, p.name, oi.quantity * oi.unit_price AS revenue FROM order_items oi JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.product_id;"
  hint="Multiply quantity by unit_price after joining order_items to products."
/>

### 6 · The full report (four tables)

<SqlExercise
  task="Build the report for SHIPPED orders only: customer full name, order_id, product name, and line revenue (quantity * unit_price). This joins all four tables."
  starter={`SELECT c.first_name || ' ' || c.last_name AS customer,\n       o.order_id,\n       p.name,\n       oi.quantity * oi.unit_price AS revenue\nFROM customers c\nJOIN orders o      ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id\nJOIN order_items oi ON o.order_id = oi.order_id\nJOIN products p     ON oi.product_id = p.product_id\nWHERE `}
  expected="SELECT c.first_name || ' ' || c.last_name AS customer, o.order_id, p.name, oi.quantity * oi.unit_price AS revenue FROM customers c JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id JOIN order_items oi ON o.order_id = oi.order_id JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.product_id WHERE o.status = 'shipped';"
  hint="Chain the joins customers→orders→order_items→products, then filter WHERE o.status = 'shipped'."
/>

## Wrap-up

You joined four tables into a single coherent report and learned to use `LEFT JOIN … IS
NULL` to find the *absences* (customers without orders, products without sales) — often the
most interesting rows of all.
