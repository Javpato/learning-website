# Project 1 — First reports

<Accroche>« You've learned to query. Now answer real questions about the store. »</Accroche>

This first project uses **only Chapter 1** skills — `SELECT`, `FROM`, `WHERE`, `ORDER BY`,
`DISTINCT` and `LIMIT`. You are the new data analyst at the sample store; the manager has
sent you a short list of reports. Write a query for each, run it, and press **Check
answer**.

<SqlSchema />

## Report 1 — Product price list

> "Give me every product's name and price, most expensive first."

<SqlExercise
  task="Name and price of every product, ordered from most to least expensive."
  starter="SELECT "
  expected="SELECT name, price FROM products ORDER BY price DESC;"
  hint="Two columns from products, then ORDER BY price DESC."
/>

## Report 2 — Which markets are we in?

> "Which countries do our customers come from? No duplicates."

<SqlExercise
  task="List each distinct customer country."
  starter="SELECT "
  expected="SELECT DISTINCT country FROM customers;"
  hint="DISTINCT collapses repeated values."
/>

## Report 3 — Out of stock

> "Which products are completely out of stock?"

<SqlExercise
  task="Show the name of every product whose stock is 0."
  starter="SELECT name FROM products"
  expected="SELECT name FROM products WHERE stock = 0;"
  hint="Filter with WHERE stock = 0."
/>

## Report 4 — Cheap impulse buys

> "Show our five cheapest products — name and price — for a checkout promo."

<SqlExercise
  task="The 5 cheapest products, showing name and price, cheapest first."
  starter="SELECT name, price FROM products"
  expected="SELECT name, price FROM products ORDER BY price ASC LIMIT 5;"
  hint="ORDER BY price ASC, then LIMIT 5."
/>

## Report 5 — Newest customers

> "Who are our three most recent sign-ups? I want their full names."

<SqlExercise
  task="First and last name of the 3 most recently signed-up customers."
  starter="SELECT first_name, last_name FROM customers"
  expected="SELECT first_name, last_name FROM customers ORDER BY signup_date DESC LIMIT 3;"
  hint="signup_date is an ISO date string, so ORDER BY signup_date DESC sorts newest first."
/>

## Wrap-up

<Collapsible title="Reflection: why did ORDER BY signup_date work even though it's text?">

The dates are stored as `'YYYY-MM-DD'` strings. That format sorts **lexicographically**
the same way it sorts chronologically — which is exactly why ISO date strings are so
convenient. You'll meet real date functions in Chapter 5.

</Collapsible>
