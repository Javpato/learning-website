# Rank of a linear map

<Toc />

<p className="accroche">Rank counts how many independent directions survive the transformation.</p>

## Definition

For a linear map $u\colon E\to F$,

$$
\operatorname{rank}(u)=\dim\operatorname{Im}(u).
$$

For a matrix, this is the dimension of the span of its columns, or equivalently of its rows.

## Rank-nullity theorem

If $E$ is finite-dimensional,

$$
\dim E=\dim\ker(u)+\operatorname{rank}(u).
$$

The kernel measures the information lost; rank measures the information retained.

## Practical computation

Elementary row operations preserve rank. Row-reduce the matrix and count its pivots. Rank is also the largest size of a nonzero minor.

For

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\
2&4&6\\
0&1&1
\end{pmatrix},
$$

the second row is twice the first, while the first and third are independent, so $\operatorname{rank}(A)=2$.

## Consequences

- $u$ is injective if and only if $\operatorname{rank}(u)=\dim E$.
- $u$ is surjective if and only if $\operatorname{rank}(u)=\dim F$.
- For a square matrix $A$, full rank, invertibility, and nonzero determinant are equivalent.

## Common pitfalls

The number of nonzero columns is not the rank: they may be dependent. A spanning family may contain far more vectors than the dimension of its span.
