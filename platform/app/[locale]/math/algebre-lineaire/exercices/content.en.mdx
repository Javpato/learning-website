## Exercise 1 — transposition on $M_n(\mathbb R)$

Consider the endomorphism

$$
\varphi\colon M_n(\mathbb R)\to M_n(\mathbb R),
\qquad \varphi(M)=M^\mathsf T.
$$

1. Show that $\varphi$ is linear and $\varphi^2=\operatorname{Id}$.
2. Find the possible eigenvalues and prove that $\varphi$ is diagonalizable.
3. Identify its eigenspaces and compute their dimensions.
4. Deduce $\operatorname{tr}(\varphi)$ and $\det(\varphi)$.

<details>
<summary>Complete solution</summary>

### 1. Linearity and involution

For $a,b\in\mathbb R$,

$$
\varphi(aM+bN)=(aM+bN)^\mathsf T=aM^\mathsf T+bN^\mathsf T.
$$

Also, $(M^\mathsf T)^\mathsf T=M$, so $\varphi^2=\operatorname{Id}$.

### 2. Spectrum and diagonalization

The polynomial $X^2-1=(X-1)(X+1)$ annihilates $\varphi$. It splits into distinct roots, so $\varphi$ is diagonalizable and its spectrum is contained in $\{-1,1\}$.

### 3. Eigenspaces

$$
E_1=\ker(\varphi-I)=\mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb R),\qquad
E_{-1}=\ker(\varphi+I)=\mathrm{A}_n(\mathbb R).
$$

A symmetric matrix is determined by its $n$ diagonal entries and its $n(n-1)/2$ entries above the diagonal:

$$
\dim E_1=\frac{n(n+1)}2.
$$

A skew-symmetric matrix has zero diagonal and is determined by its strictly upper entries:

$$
\dim E_{-1}=\frac{n(n-1)}2.
$$

The explicit decomposition

$$
M=\frac{M+M^\mathsf T}{2}+\frac{M-M^\mathsf T}{2}
$$

confirms that $M_n(\mathbb R)=E_1\oplus E_{-1}$.

### 4. Trace and determinant

In an eigenbasis, the matrix of $\varphi$ contains $n(n+1)/2$ copies of $1$ and $n(n-1)/2$ copies of $-1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}(\varphi)=
\frac{n(n+1)}2-\frac{n(n-1)}2=n},
$$

$$
\boxed{\det(\varphi)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}}.
$$

## Exercise 2 — differentiation on $\mathbb R_n[X]$

Let $D(P)=P'$ on $\mathbb R_n[X]$, in the basis $(1,X,\ldots,X^n)$.

1. Write the matrix of $D$.
2. Compute $D^k(X^j)$.
3. Find $\ker(D^k)$, $\operatorname{Im}(D^k)$, and the rank of $D^k$.
4. Prove that $D$ is nilpotent of index $n+1$.
5. Is $D$ diagonalizable?

<details>
<summary>Complete solution</summary>

### 1. Matrix

Since $D(1)=0$ and $D(X^j)=jX^{j-1}$,

$$
\operatorname{Mat}(D)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&2&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&0&n\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

### 2. Powers

For $k\leq j$,

$$
D^k(X^j)=\frac{j!}{(j-k)!}X^{j-k},
$$

and $D^k(X^j)=0$ when $k>j$.

### 3. Kernel, image, and rank

For $0\leq k\leq n+1$,

$$
\ker(D^k)=\mathbb R_{k-1}[X],
\qquad
\operatorname{Im}(D^k)=\mathbb R_{n-k}[X].
$$

With the natural endpoint conventions,

$$
\dim\ker(D^k)=k,\qquad \operatorname{rank}(D^k)=n+1-k.
$$

Rank-nullity checks: $k+(n+1-k)=n+1$.

### 4. Exact index

$D^{n+1}=0$ on all of $\mathbb R_n[X]$, while $D^n(X^n)=n!\neq0$. Thus the index of nilpotency is exactly $n+1$.

### 5. Diagonalization

$D$ is nonzero and nilpotent for $n\geq1$. Its only eigenvalue is $0$; if it were diagonalizable, it would be zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{D\text{ is not diagonalizable for }n\geq1.}
$$

</details>
