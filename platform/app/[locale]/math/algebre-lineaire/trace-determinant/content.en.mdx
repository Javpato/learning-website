# Trace and determinant

<Toc />

<p className="accroche">Trace adds the eigenvalues; determinant multiplies them.</p>

## Two invariants

For a square matrix $A=(a_{ij})$,

$$
\operatorname{tr}(A)=\sum_i a_{ii},
\qquad
\det(A)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\varepsilon(\sigma)\prod_i a_{i,\sigma(i)}.
$$

Both quantities are invariant under similarity: if $B=P^{-1}AP$, then $\operatorname{tr}(B)=\operatorname{tr}(A)$ and $\det(B)=\det(A)$.

## Spectral interpretation

Over $\mathbb C$, counting multiplicities,

$$
\operatorname{tr}(A)=\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_n,
\qquad
\det(A)=\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_n.
$$

The determinant also measures the signed volume-scaling factor. It is zero exactly when the map collapses at least one direction.

## Transposition as an operator

On $M_n(\mathbb R)$, let $\varphi(M)=M^\mathsf T$. Every matrix splits uniquely as

$$
M=\underbrace{\frac{M+M^\mathsf T}{2}}_{S\text{ symmetric}}
+
\underbrace{\frac{M-M^\mathsf T}{2}}_{K\text{ skew-symmetric}}.
$$

The eigenvalue is $1$ on $\mathrm{Sym}_n$ and $-1$ on $\mathrm{A}_n$. Moreover,

$$
\dim\mathrm{Sym}_n=\frac{n(n+1)}2,\qquad
\dim\mathrm{A}_n=\frac{n(n-1)}2.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\operatorname{tr}(\varphi)=n,\qquad
\det(\varphi)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}.
$$

## Essential properties

- $\operatorname{tr}(A+B)=\operatorname{tr}(A)+\operatorname{tr}(B)$.
- $\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)$.
- $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$.
- $A$ is invertible if and only if $\det(A)\neq0$.

## Common pitfalls

Trace is not multiplicative, and determinant is not additive. Do not confuse $\det(M)$, attached to one matrix $M$, with $\det(\varphi)$, attached to the transposition operator on the $n^2$-dimensional matrix space.
