## How to use this toolkit

This module will stack new ideas — scalar fields, gradients, phase portraits — on L1 skills which must have become *core skills*: differentiate, integrate, solve a small ODE, manipulate a $2\times2$ system. If one of these actions still requires thought, it will consume the attention you need for the new idea. This lesson is therefore a *diagnosis*, not a judgment: it only helps you identify what to review, and where.

## Required core skills

You should be able, without hesitation:

- solve $ax + b = 0$ and a linear system $2\times2$;
- differentiate exponentials, logarithms, trigonometric functions and their compositions;
- integrate simple powers, exponentials and trigonometric functions;
- solve $y' = ay$, $y' + ay = b$ and ODEs with separable variables;
- manipulate vectors, dot products, norms and determinants $2\times2$;
- factor a quadratic polynomial and interpret its discriminant.

If just one of these points makes you doubt, write it down: the diagnosis below will tell you if it is superficial rust or a real hole.

## The minimal diagnosis

Without notes or a calculator, solve:

1. $y' + 3y = 6$, with $y(0) = 5$.
2. Find the eigenvalues of $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. Determine the tangent line to $f(x) = \ln(1 + x^2)$ at $x = 1$.
4. Convert $(x, y) = (-\sqrt3,\ 1)$ to polar coordinates.
5. Classify the quadratic form $x^2 - 4xy + 5y^2$.

Take the time to write your five answers before opening the corrigé (worked solutions).

<Collapsible title="Detailed answers">

**1. First-order linear ODE.** The homogeneous equation $y' + 3y = 0$ gives $y_h = C e^{-3t}$. A constant particular solution satisfies $3y_p = 6$, therefore $y_p = 2$. General solution: $y = 2 + Ce^{-3t}$. The condition $y(0) = 5$ gives $C = 3$:

$$
y(t) = 2 + 3e^{-3t}.
$$

*Verification:* $y' = -9e^{-3t}$ and $3y = 6 + 9e^{-3t}$, the sum of which is indeed $6$; and $y(0) = 2 + 3 = 5$.

**2. Eigenvalues.** The characteristic polynomial is

$$
\det\begin{pmatrix} 2-\lambda & 1 \\ 1 & 2-\lambda \end{pmatrix}
= (2-\lambda)^2 - 1 = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2 - \lambda = \pm 1,
$$

hence $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = 3$. *Verification:* the trace is $1 + 3 = 4$ and the determinant $1\times3 = 3 = 4 - 1$.

**3. Tangent.** $f(1) = \ln 2$ and $f'(x) = \dfrac{2x}{1+x^2}$, therefore $f'(1) = 1$:

$$
y = \ln 2 + (x - 1).
$$

**4. Polars — watch out for the quadrant.** $r = \sqrt{3 + 1} = 2$. The naive calculation $\arctan(y/x) = \arctan(-1/\sqrt3) = -\pi/6$ is **wrong** here: the point $(-\sqrt3, 1)$ is in the *second* quadrant ($x$ negative, $y$ positive), adjust by half a turn:

$$
\theta = -\frac{\pi}{6} + \pi = \frac{5\pi}{6} \pmod{2\pi}.
$$

*Verification:* $2\cos(5\pi/6) = -\sqrt3$ and $2\sin(5\pi/6) = 1$. This trap will come back in force in lesson 1.

**5. Quadratic form.** The associated symmetric matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$ (the cross-term coefficient $-4$ is split into two $-2$). The first leading principal minor equals $1$, positive, and the determinant equals $5 - 4 = 1$, also positive: the form is **positive definite** — it is a “bowl” which only takes strictly positive values outside the origin.

</Collapsible>

<Pitfall>

**The $\arctan(y/x)$ reflex without looking at the quadrant.** This is the most frequent error at the beginning of the module: $\arctan$ only returns angles between $-\pi/2$ and $\pi/2$, so any point with negative $x$ receives an incorrect angle if we do not correct $\pm\pi$. Always draw a mini-diagram of the point before concluding.

</Pitfall>

## Interactive self-test

Same five questions, instant version — answer on paper first, then check here.

<Quiz title="Quick check — optional, as always">

  <QItem>

    What is the solution of $y' + 3y = 6$ with $y(0) = 5$?

    <QOption id="a" correct>$y = 2 + 3e^{-3t}$</QOption>
    <QOption id="b">$y = 5e^{-3t}$</QOption>
    <QOption id="c">$y = 6 - e^{-3t}$</QOption>
    <QFeedback of="a">

      Yes: constant particular solution $y_p = 2$, homogeneous solution $Ce^{-3t}$, and the initial condition fixes $C = 3$.

    </QFeedback>
    <QFeedback of="b">

      This is the solution to the *homogeneous* equation $y' + 3y = 0$ — you forgot the right-hand side $6$, which imposes the particular solution $y_p = 2$.

    </QFeedback>
    <QFeedback of="c">

      Check the initial condition: this candidate gives $y(0) = 5$, but $y' + 3y = e^{-3t}\cdot3 + 18 - 3e^{-3t}$… the calculation does not check out. Repeat with “homogeneous + particular”.

    </QFeedback>
    <QExplain>

      Standard method: particular solution (here constant, $y_p = b/a$) plus homogeneous solution $Ce^{-at}$, then initial condition. This same pattern will reappear for first-order chemical kinetics.

    </QExplain>

  </QItem>
  <QItem>

    What is the slope of the tangent to $f(x) = \ln(1 + x^2)$ at $x = 1$?

    <QOption id="a">$\tfrac12$</QOption>
    <QOption id="b" correct>$1$</QOption>
    <QOption id="c">$2$</QOption>
    <QFeedback of="a">

      You may have derived $\ln$ without the chain rule: you must multiply $\frac{1}{1+x^2}$ by the *internal* derivative $2x$.

    </QFeedback>
    <QFeedback of="b">

      Correct: $f'(x) = \dfrac{2x}{1+x^2}$, therefore $f'(1) = \dfrac22 = 1$.

    </QFeedback>
    <QFeedback of="c">

      $2$ is the inner derivative $2x$ at $x=1$… multiplied by nothing. The factor $\frac{1}{1+x^2} = \frac12$ is missing.

    </QFeedback>
    <QExplain>

      chain rule: $(\ln u)' = u'/u$. The full tangent is $y = \ln 2 + (x-1)$ — exactly the calculation that the *tangent plane* from Lesson 4 will generalize.

    </QExplain>

  </QItem>
  <QNumeric answer={3} tolerance={0}>

    What is the **largest** eigenvalue of $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$?

    <QExplain>

      $(2-\lambda)^2 - 1 = 0$ gives $\lambda = 1$ or $\lambda = 3$. Quick check: trace $= 4 = 1 + 3$, determinant $= 3 = 1\times3$.

    </QExplain>

  </QNumeric>
  <QNumeric answer={2} tolerance={0}>

    What is the radius $r$ of the polar coordinates of the point $(-\sqrt3,\ 1)$?

    <QExplain>

      $r = \sqrt{(-\sqrt3)^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt4 = 2$. And the angle is $\theta = 5\pi/6$ — not $-\pi/6$: the point is in the second quadrant, you have to adjust $\arctan$ by $+\pi$.

    </QExplain>

  </QNumeric>
  <QItem>

    The quadratic form $x^2 - 4xy + 5y^2$ is…

    <QOption id="a" correct>positive definite</QOption>
    <QOption id="b">indefinite (saddle type)</QOption>
    <QOption id="c">negative definite</QOption>
    <QFeedback of="a">

      Yes: leading principal minors $1$ and $5 - 4 = 1$, both positive. Other reading: $x^2 - 4xy + 5y^2 = (x - 2y)^2 + y^2$, sum of two squares.

    </QFeedback>
    <QFeedback of="b">

      A saddle would require a *negative* determinant of the associated matrix. This is $\det = 1\cdot5 - (-2)^2 = 1$, positive. Please note: the cross-term coefficient $-4$ is split into two $-2$ off-diagonal.

    </QFeedback>
    <QFeedback of="c">

      Test $y = 0$: the form is $x^2$, strictly positive — it cannot be negative definite.

    </QFeedback>
    <QExplain>

      Leading-principal-minor criterion for $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$: $1$ and $1$ positive, therefore positive definite. This classification is the heart of lesson 6 (critical points and Hessian).

    </QExplain>

  </QItem>

## What to do with your score

- **5/5 without hesitation**: go straight to lesson 1 — you're ready.
- **One or two hesitations**: revise the specific point (first-order ODE, chain rule, polar quadrant, leading principal minors) then repeat the question — ten minutes is often enough.
- **Three or more questions you find difficult**: take a real L1 revision session before tackling the module; it is better to lose two days now than to suffer ten weeks afterwards.

## Lesson summary

<KeyResults title="The essentials">

- The module assumes six families of core skills: linear equations and systems, differentiation, integration, simple ODEs, vector algebra $2\times2$, trinomials.
- ODE $y' + ay = b$: solution $= b/a + Ce^{-at}$, then initial condition.
- Eigenvalues $2\times2$: characteristic polynomial, checks using the trace and determinant.
- Polar: $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, and **always** correct $\arctan(y/x)$ according to the quadrant.
- Quadratic form: leading principal minors positive $\Rightarrow$ positive definite.
- A diagnosis is used to target revision — not to judge.
