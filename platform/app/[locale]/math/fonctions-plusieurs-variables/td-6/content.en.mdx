<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td6-01">

<Statement>

Solve
$X'=\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\2&3\end{pmatrix}X$ with $X(0)=(1,0)^\top$.

</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>The characteristic polynomial has roots $2$ and $5$.</Hint>
<Hint>Decompose the initial condition in the eigenbasis.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>

$X(t)=\frac13e^{2t}(1,-2)^\top+\frac23e^{5t}(1,1)^\top$.

</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

Eigenvectors are $v_1=(1,-2)$ for $\lambda_1=2$ and
$v_2=(1,1)$ for $\lambda_2=5$. Write
$(1,0)=c_1v_1+c_2v_2$. The system $c_1+c_2=1$,
$-2c_1+c_2=0$ gives $c_1=1/3$, $c_2=2/3$. Each eigencomponent evolves
as $e^{\lambda t}$, which gives the solution.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Using the eigenvalues as initial coefficients.
- Forgetting to impose $X(0)$ after writing the general solution.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td6-02">

<Statement>

Solve
$X'=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}X$,
$X(0)=(a,b)^\top$.

</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>Write $A=-I+N$ with $N^2=0$.</Hint>
<Hint>$e^{tN}=I+tN$.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>$X(t)=e^{-t}(a+bt,b)^\top$.</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

Since $-I$ and $N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ commute,

$$
e^{tA}=e^{-t}e^{tN}=e^{-t}(I+tN)
=e^{-t}\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Multiplication by $(a,b)^\top$ gives the result. The polynomial factor
$t$ is the signature of the Jordan block.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Looking for two independent eigenvectors even though the matrix is defective.
- Forgetting the factor $e^{-t}$ on the term $bt$.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td6-03">

<Statement>

Construct a real $2\times2$ matrix for each of the following portraits:
stable spiral, unstable node, saddle with invariant directions $y=\pm x$,
and defective stable node.

</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>For a spiral, combine dilation $aI$ and rotation $bJ$.</Hint>
<Hint>To impose $y=\pm x$, choose these vectors as an eigenbasis.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>

Possible answers are

$$
\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\2&-1\end{pmatrix},\
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix},\
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\
\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

The first has eigenvalues $-1\pm2i$: stable spiral. The second has
two positive eigenvalues: unstable node. The third maps
$(1,1)$ to itself and $(1,-1)$ to its opposite: a saddle with the requested
lines. The last has the single eigenvalue $-1$, only one eigenspace,
and a Jordan block: defective stable node.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Confusing “stable” with positive eigenvalues.
- Giving a diagonal matrix for the defective case.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td6-04">

<Statement>

For $A\xrightarrow{k_1}B\xrightarrow{k_2}C$, with
$A(0)=A_0$, $B(0)=0$, and $k_1\ne k_2$, find $A(t)$, $B(t)$, and the time at which
$B$ is maximal.

</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>First solve $A'=-k_1A$.</Hint>
<Hint>The equation $B'+k_2B=k_1A_0e^{-k_1t}$ is linear.</Hint>
<Hint>Set $B'(t)=0$ for the maximum.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>

$A=A_0e^{-k_1t}$,
$B=\dfrac{k_1A_0}{k_2-k_1}(e^{-k_1t}-e^{-k_2t})$, and
$t_{\max}=\dfrac{\ln(k_2/k_1)}{k_2-k_1}$.

</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

The first equation is immediate. The integrating factor $e^{k_2t}$ gives

$$
B(t)=e^{-k_2t}\int_0^t k_1A_0e^{(k_2-k_1)s}\,ds,
$$

which gives the stated formula. The condition $B'=k_1A-k_2B=0$ reduces to
$k_2e^{-k_2t}=k_1e^{-k_1t}$, so
$t_{\max}=\ln(k_2/k_1)/(k_2-k_1)$, which is positive for any two distinct
positive rates.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Forgetting the initial condition $B(0)=0$.
- Believing that the formula for $t_{\max}$ becomes negative when $k_2<k_1$.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td6-05">

<Statement>

For $A=\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\b&a\end{pmatrix}$, prove that
$e^{At}=e^{at}\begin{pmatrix}\cos bt&-\sin bt\\\sin bt&\cos bt\end{pmatrix}$.

</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>Write $A=aI+bJ$, where $J^2=-I$.</Hint>
<Hint>Separate the even and odd powers in the series for $e^{btJ}$.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>

$e^{At}=e^{at}(\cos(bt)I+\sin(bt)J)$, which is the requested matrix.

</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

$I$ and $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ commute, so
$e^{At}=e^{at}e^{btJ}$. Since $J^{2n}=(-1)^nI$ and
$J^{2n+1}=(-1)^nJ$, the exponential series becomes
$\cos(bt)I+\sin(bt)J$. This factor is a rotation, while $e^{at}$
contracts if $a<0$ and expands if $a>0$.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Replacing $e^{A+B}$ with $e^Ae^B$ without checking commutation.
- Losing the minus sign in the upper-right entry.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>
