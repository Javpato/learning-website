<ExamProblem title="Exercise 1 — Surface and gradient" points={7}>

Let the function be

$$
f(x,y) = x\,e^{-(x^2+y^2)} .
$$

1. What symmetries does the <Terme id="graphe">surface</Terme> $z = f(x,y)$ have? (Study
   $f(-x,y)$ and $f(x,-y)$.)
2. Determine all the <Terme id="point-critique">critical points</Terme> of $f$.
3. Classify each critical point using the <Terme id="hessienne">Hessian</Terme>. Are these <Terme id="extremum-local">extrema</Terme>
   global?
4. Write the equation of the <Terme id="plan-tangent">tangent plane</Terme> to the graph of $f$ at $(1, 0)$.
5. Determine the <Terme id="ensemble-de-niveau">level set</Terme> $f = 0$ and describe it geometrically.

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème (marking scheme)">

**1. Symmetries (1 pt).**

$$
f(-x,y) = -f(x,y),
\qquad
f(x,-y) = f(x,y) .
$$

The function is **odd in $x$** (the surface is antisymmetric with
respect to the plane $x = 0$) and **even in $y$** (symmetric with respect to the plane
$y = 0$). It is therefore enough to study the quadrant $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$.

**2. Critical points (2 pts).** With the common factor
$e^{-(x^2+y^2)} > 0$:

$$
f_x = \bigl(1 - 2x^2\bigr)\,e^{-(x^2+y^2)},
\qquad
f_y = -2xy\,e^{-(x^2+y^2)} .
$$

$f_x = 0$ imposes $x = \pm 1/\sqrt2$ (so $x \neq 0$), and then $f_y = 0$
imposes $y = 0$. The critical points are

$$
\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2},\,0\right)
\quad\text{et}\quad
\left(-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2},\,0\right).
$$

**3. Classification (2 pts).** The second derivatives are

$$
f_{xx} = \bigl(4x^3 - 6x\bigr)e^{-(x^2+y^2)},
\quad
f_{yy} = \bigl(4xy^2 - 2x\bigr)e^{-(x^2+y^2)},
\quad
f_{xy} = -2y\bigl(1-2x^2\bigr)e^{-(x^2+y^2)} .
$$

At $\bigl(1/\sqrt2,\,0\bigr)$: $f_{xx} = -2\sqrt2\,e^{-1/2}$,
$f_{yy} = -\sqrt2\,e^{-1/2}$, $f_{xy} = 0$, hence

$$
D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2 = 4e^{-1} > 0,
\qquad f_{xx} < 0 :
$$

it is a **local maximum**, with value $f = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}e^{-1/2}
\approx 0{,}429$. By oddness in $x$, $\bigl(-1/\sqrt2,\,0\bigr)$ is a
**local minimum** with value $-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}e^{-1/2}$.

They are **global**: $f(x,y) \to 0$ as $x^2+y^2 \to +\infty$, $f$ is
<Terme id="continuite">continuous</Terme> and has no other critical point, so the supremum and infimum are
attained at these two points.

**4. Tangent plane at $(1,0)$ (1 pt).** $f(1,0) = e^{-1}$,
$f_x(1,0) = (1-2)e^{-1} = -e^{-1}$, $f_y(1,0) = 0$:

$$
z = e^{-1} - e^{-1}(x-1) = e^{-1}\,(2 - x) .
$$

**5. Level set $f = 0$ (1 pt).** Since $e^{-(x^2+y^2)} \neq 0$
everywhere,

$$
f(x,y) = 0 \iff x = 0 :
$$

it is **the entire $y$-axis** (the line $x = 0$), a <Terme id="ligne-de-niveau">level curve</Terme> that
separates the “bump” ($x > 0$) from the “dip” ($x < 0$).

**What the examiner rewards.** Factoring the strictly
positive exponential before solving $\nabla f = 0$ (instead of expanding), using
symmetry to infer the nature of the second critical point without repeating the
calculation, and using behavior at infinity to move from “local” to
“global.”

</Collapsible>

</ExamProblem>

<ExamProblem title="Exercise 2 — Uncertainty in an Arrhenius law" points={5}>

The rate constant follows the Arrhenius law

$$
k = A\,e^{-E/(RT)},
$$

where $A$ (pre-exponential factor) and $E$ (activation energy) are estimated
by a regression — and are therefore **correlated** — while $T$ is measured independently.

1. Establish the <Terme id="differentielle-totale">differential</Terme> of $\ln k$.
2. Write the variance $\sigma^2_{\ln k}$ in matrix form
   $\mathbf g^\top \Sigma\,\mathbf g$, specifying $\mathbf g$ and $\Sigma$.
3. Expand this expression when the only nonzero <Terme id="covariance">covariance</Terme> is
   $\operatorname{Cov}(A, E)$.

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème">

**1. Logarithmic differential (2 pts).**

$$
\ln k = \ln A - \frac{E}{RT}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d\ln k = \frac{dA}{A} - \frac{dE}{RT} + \frac{E}{RT^2}\,dT .
$$

As in Exercise 3 of the contrôle continu (continuous assessment), the term $E/(RT)$ depends on
$E$ *and* $T$: it produces two contributions.

**2. Matrix form (2 pts).** The sensitivity vector of
$\ln k$ with respect to $(A, E, T)$ is

$$
\mathbf g =
\begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{1}{A} \\[4pt] -\dfrac{1}{RT} \\[4pt] \dfrac{E}{RT^2} \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Sigma =
\begin{pmatrix}
\sigma_A^2 & \operatorname{Cov}(A,E) & 0 \\
\operatorname{Cov}(A,E) & \sigma_E^2 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \sigma_T^2
\end{pmatrix},
$$

where the zeros express the independence of $T$ from the fitted pair
$(A, E)$. Then

$$
\sigma^2_{\ln k} = \mathbf g^\top \Sigma\,\mathbf g .
$$

**3. Expansion (1 pt).**

$$
\sigma^2_{\ln k}
= \frac{\sigma_A^2}{A^2}
+ \frac{\sigma_E^2}{R^2T^2}
+ \frac{E^2}{R^2T^4}\,\sigma_T^2
- \frac{2\operatorname{Cov}(A,E)}{A\,R\,T} .
$$

The cross term is $2\,g_A\,g_E\operatorname{Cov}(A,E)$ with
$g_A = 1/A$ and $g_E = -1/(RT)$, hence the **minus** sign before the
covariance. In an Arrhenius regression, $\ln A$ and $E$ are almost
always **positively** correlated: ignoring this term then overestimates
the <Terme id="incertitude-type">uncertainty</Terme>.

**What the examiner rewards.** Consistency among the three forms
(differential, matrix form, expanded form), the block
structure of $\Sigma$ justified physically, and rigorous tracking of the sign
of the cross term.

</Collapsible>

</ExamProblem>

<ExamProblem title="Exercise 3 — Linear differential system" points={8}>

Consider the <Terme id="systeme-differentiel">system</Terme>

$$
X' = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ -5 & -2 \end{pmatrix} X,
\qquad X = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}.
$$

1. Calculate the <Terme id="valeur-propre">eigenvalues</Terme> of the matrix.
2. Deduce the general **real** solution of the system.
3. Study the <Terme id="stabilite">stability</Terme> of the <Terme id="point-d-equilibre">equilibrium</Terme> $(0,0)$ and name the
   <Terme id="portrait-de-phase">phase portrait</Terme>.
4. Determine the direction of rotation of the <Terme id="trajectoire">trajectories</Terme>.

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème">

**1. Eigenvalues (2 pts).** The <Terme id="polynome-caracteristique">characteristic polynomial</Terme> is

$$
\det\begin{pmatrix} -2-\lambda & 1 \\ -5 & -2-\lambda \end{pmatrix}
= (\lambda+2)^2 + 5 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda = -2 \pm i\sqrt5 .
$$

**2. General real solution (3 pts).** For $\lambda = -2 + i\sqrt5$, an
<Terme id="vecteur-propre">eigenvector</Terme> is obtained from the first row: $(-i\sqrt5)\,v_1 + v_2 = 0$, so
$v = (1,\ i\sqrt5)$. The complex solution
$e^{(-2+i\sqrt5)t}(1,\ i\sqrt5)$ gives, by taking real and
imaginary parts:

$$
X(t) = e^{-2t}\left[
c_1 \begin{pmatrix} \cos(\sqrt5\,t) \\ -\sqrt5\,\sin(\sqrt5\,t) \end{pmatrix}
+ c_2 \begin{pmatrix} \sin(\sqrt5\,t) \\ \sqrt5\,\cos(\sqrt5\,t) \end{pmatrix}
\right],
\qquad c_1, c_2 \in \mathbb R .
$$

(Quick check: at $t = 0$, $X = (c_1,\ \sqrt5\,c_2)$, and the two
basis solutions are independent.)

**3. Stability (1 pt).** $\operatorname{Re}\lambda = -2 < 0$: all
solutions tend to $0$ — the origin is **asymptotically stable**, and
because the eigenvalues are nonreal complex numbers, it is a
**stable <Terme id="foyer">focus (spiral)</Terme>**. In terms of invariants: $\tau = -4 < 0$,
$\Delta = 9 > 0$, $\tau^2 - 4\Delta = -20 < 0$ — the same conclusion from the
<Terme id="plan-trace-determinant">trace–determinant</Terme> plane.

**4. Direction of rotation (2 pts).** Evaluate the field at a simple point,
$X = (1, 0)$:

$$
X' = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

On the positive $x$-axis, the field points downward ($y' = -5 < 0$): the
rotation is **clockwise**. The portrait is therefore a stable
spiral traversed clockwise.

**What the examiner rewards.** A clean passage from complex to real
(real and imaginary parts of *one* complex solution — no complex constants
in the final answer), the stability conclusion drawn from the sign
of $\operatorname{Re}\lambda$, and testing the direction of rotation by
evaluating the field at one point (a foolproof ten-second method).
