<ExamProblem title="Problem A — Energy landscape" points={12}>

For two real parameters $a$ and $b$, consider the potential

$$
V(x,y) = \frac14 x^4 - \frac a2 x^2 + \frac12 y^2 + b\,xy .
$$

1. **(4 pts)** Calculate $\nabla V$ and show that the critical points
   satisfy $y = -bx$ and $x\bigl(x^2 - (a + b^2)\bigr) = 0$. Discuss the
   number of critical points according to the sign of $a + b^2$.
2. **(4 pts)** Write the Hessian of $V$ and classify all the critical
   points (distinguish the cases $a + b^2 > 0$, $a + b^2 < 0$, and
   $a + b^2 = 0$).
3. **(4 pts)** For $a = 1$ and $b = 1$, diagonalize the Hessian at the
   nonzero critical point with positive abscissa: eigenvalues, eigenvectors,
   and interpretation (stiff and soft directions of the well).

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème (marking scheme)">

**1. Critical points (4 pts).**

$$
\nabla V = \bigl(x^3 - a x + b y,\ \ y + b x\bigr).
$$

*(1 pt)* $V_y = 0$ gives $y = -bx$; *(1 pt)* substituting into $V_x = 0$:

$$
x^3 - ax - b^2 x = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\bigl(x^2 - (a + b^2)\bigr) = 0 .
$$

*(2 pts)* Discussion:

- if $a + b^2 \le 0$: the only solution is $x = 0$, so there is **one unique
  critical point**, the origin;
- if $a + b^2 > 0$: there are three critical points, the origin and

$$
P_\pm = \Bigl(\pm\sqrt{a+b^2},\ \mp b\sqrt{a+b^2}\Bigr).
$$

**2. Classification (4 pts).** *(1 pt)* The Hessian is

$$
H_V(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} 3x^2 - a & b \\ b & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D = (3x^2 - a) - b^2 .
$$

*(1,5 pts)* **At the origin**: $D = -(a + b^2)$.

- If $a + b^2 > 0$: $D < 0$, so the origin is a **saddle point**.
- If $a + b^2 < 0$: $D > 0$ and $V_{xx} = -a > 0$ (because
  $a < -b^2 \le 0$): **local minimum**.
- If $a + b^2 = 0$: $D = 0$, so the test is degenerate; but then
  $V = \frac14 x^4 + \frac12 (y + bx)^2 \ge 0$, and it vanishes only at $(0,0)$:
  **global minimum** (degenerate in the sense of the test).

*(1,5 pts)* **At the points $P_\pm$** (case $a + b^2 > 0$): with
$x^2 = a + b^2$,

$$
V_{xx} = 3(a+b^2) - a = 2a + 3b^2,
\qquad
D = (2a + 3b^2) - b^2 = 2(a + b^2) > 0,
$$

and $V_{xx} = 2(a+b^2) + b^2 > 0$: the two points $P_\pm$ are
**local minima** (in fact global, because $V \to +\infty$ at infinity). This is
the “double-well” landscape: two stable structures separated by a
transition state at the origin.

**3. Diagonalization for $a = b = 1$ (4 pts).** *(1 pt)* Then
$a + b^2 = 2$, the requested point is $P_+ = (\sqrt2,\ -\sqrt2)$, and

$$
H_V(P_+) = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

*(1,5 pts)* Characteristic polynomial:
$\lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 4 = 0$, hence

$$
\lambda_\pm = 3 \pm \sqrt5,
\qquad
\lambda_+ \approx 5{,}24,
\quad
\lambda_- \approx 0{,}76 .
$$

Both eigenvalues are strictly positive: the minimum test is
confirmed. *(1 pt)* Eigenvectors (first row
$(5-\lambda)v_1 + v_2 = 0$):

$$
v_+ = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ \sqrt5 - 2 \end{pmatrix}
\ (\lambda_+ = 3+\sqrt5),
\qquad
v_- = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -\sqrt5 - 2 \end{pmatrix}
\ (\lambda_- = 3-\sqrt5),
$$

orthogonal to each other (symmetric matrix): in the associated orthonormal
basis, $H_V(P_+) = P\operatorname{diag}(3+\sqrt5,\ 3-\sqrt5)P^\top$.

*(0,5 pt)* **Interpretation.** Near $P_+$,
$V \approx V(P_+) + \frac12\bigl(\lambda_+ u^2 + \lambda_- w^2\bigr)$ in
the eigenvector coordinates $(u, w)$: the direction $v_+$ is the
**stiff** direction (hard mode, curvature $\approx 5{,}24$), and the direction $v_-$ is the
**soft** direction (soft mode, curvature $\approx 0{,}76$) — exactly the
structure of normal modes around a molecular equilibrium.

**What the examiner rewards.** Reducing the system
$\nabla V = 0$ by substitution (rather than solving at random), a
complete case-by-case discussion — including the degenerate case treated by
factorization — and the final link between Hessian eigenvalues and
directional stiffness.

<ExamProblem title="Problem B — Coupled kinetics" points={14}>

Consider the reaction mechanism

$$
\mathrm A \underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}} \mathrm B,
\qquad
\mathrm B \xrightarrow{\ k_2\ } \mathrm C,
$$

with rate constants $k_1, k_{-1}, k_2 > 0$. Write
$x = [\mathrm A]$ and $y = [\mathrm B]$.

1. **(3 pts)** Write the differential system satisfied by $(x, y)$ in the
   form $X' = MX$ and give the matrix $M$.
2. **(3 pts)** Without calculating the eigenvalues, show that the equilibrium
   $(0,0)$ is asymptotically stable (use the trace and
   determinant).
3. **(5 pts)** Show that the eigenvalues of $M$ are real and
   strictly negative, then write the general solution of the system.
4. **(3 pts)** In the regime $k_2 \ll k_1, k_{-1}$, give an
   approximation of the slow eigenvalue and interpret the two time scales
   (fast pre-equilibrium, slow drainage toward C).

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème">

**1. Forming the system (3 pts).** Flux balance: A loses $k_1 x$ and recovers
$k_{-1} y$; B receives $k_1 x$ and loses $(k_{-1} + k_2)\,y$:

$$
\begin{cases}
x' = -k_1 x + k_{-1}\,y, \\
y' = k_1 x - (k_{-1} + k_2)\,y,
\end{cases}
\qquad
M = \begin{pmatrix} -k_1 & k_{-1} \\ k_1 & -(k_{-1}+k_2) \end{pmatrix}.
$$

**2. Stability from trace and determinant (3 pts).**

$$
\tau = \operatorname{tr} M = -(k_1 + k_{-1} + k_2) < 0,
\qquad
\Delta = \det M = k_1(k_{-1}+k_2) - k_1 k_{-1} = k_1 k_2 > 0 .
$$

*(1 pt for justification)* $\Delta > 0$ rules out a saddle point and requires
the two eigenvalues to have the same sign (if real) or be conjugates;
$\tau < 0$ then imposes a strictly negative real part on
each one: the origin is **asymptotically stable**. Chemically, everything
eventually becomes C; the concentrations of A and B vanish.

**3. Eigenvalues and general solution (5 pts).** *(2 pts)* The
discriminant of the characteristic polynomial
$\lambda^2 - \tau\lambda + \Delta$ is

$$
\tau^2 - 4\Delta = (k_1 + k_{-1} + k_2)^2 - 4k_1k_2
\ \ge\ (k_1 + k_2)^2 - 4k_1k_2 = (k_1 - k_2)^2 \ \ge\ 0,
$$

because $k_{-1} > 0$ only increases the square. The eigenvalues are
therefore **real**, and because their sum $\tau$ is negative and their product
$\Delta$ is positive, they are **both strictly negative**:

$$
\lambda_\pm = \frac{-(k_1+k_{-1}+k_2) \pm \sqrt{(k_1+k_{-1}+k_2)^2 - 4k_1k_2}}{2},
\qquad
\lambda_- \le \lambda_+ < 0 .
$$

The portrait is a **stable node**: decay without oscillation — a
mechanism of this kind cannot produce oscillating concentrations.

*(3 pts)* An eigenvector for $\lambda$ (first row
$(-k_1-\lambda)v_1 + k_{-1}v_2 = 0$) is $v_\lambda = (k_{-1},\ k_1+\lambda)$.
Hence the general solution is

$$
X(t) = c_+\,e^{\lambda_+ t}\begin{pmatrix} k_{-1} \\ k_1 + \lambda_+ \end{pmatrix}
+ c_-\,e^{\lambda_- t}\begin{pmatrix} k_{-1} \\ k_1 + \lambda_- \end{pmatrix},
$$

where the constants $c_\pm$ are fixed by the initial concentrations.

**4. Time scales (3 pts).** The eigenvalues satisfy
$\lambda_+\lambda_- = k_1k_2$ and $\lambda_+ + \lambda_- = -(k_1+k_{-1}+k_2)$.
If $k_2 \ll k_1, k_{-1}$, the fast mode carries almost the entire trace,
$\lambda_- \approx -(k_1 + k_{-1})$, and the product gives the slow mode:

$$
\lambda_+ \approx -\frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_{-1} + k_2}
\approx -\frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_{-1}} .
$$

Interpretation: over a time $\sim 1/(k_1+k_{-1})$, the exchange
A $\rightleftharpoons$ B reaches its **pre-equilibrium** (the ratio
$y/x \approx k_1/k_{-1}$ becomes fixed); then the whole system drains **slowly** toward
C with effective constant $k_{\text{eff}} = k_1k_2/(k_1+k_{-1})$ —
exactly the pre-equilibrium approximation used in chemical kinetics,
recovered here as the matrix's slow eigenvalue.

**What the examiner rewards.** A correctly signed flux balance
(question 1), the trace–determinant argument *before* any eigenvalue
calculation, an honest lower bound for the discriminant, and especially the physical
reading of question 4: connecting the slow eigenvalue to the effective rate
constant is worth more than the raw formula.

<ExamProblem title="Problem C — Hamiltonian versus damping" points={14}>

Study the oscillator

$$
x' = v,
\qquad
v' = -\omega_0^2\,x - \gamma\,v,
\qquad \omega_0 > 0,\ \gamma \ge 0 .
$$

1. **(3 pts)** Write the system in matrix form and classify
   the equilibrium $(0,0)$ according to $\gamma$ (introduce
   $\zeta = \gamma/(2\omega_0)$).
2. **(3 pts)** For $\gamma = 0$, show that the system is Hamiltonian:
   exhibit $H(x,v)$ such that $x' = \partial H/\partial v$ and
   $v' = -\partial H/\partial x$, and describe the trajectories.
3. **(3 pts)** For $\gamma > 0$, calculate the derivative of
   $E(x,v) = \frac12 v^2 + \frac12\omega_0^2 x^2$ along the trajectories
   and conclude.
4. **(3 pts)** Show that for $\gamma > 0$, the system has no
   nontrivial periodic orbit.
5. **(2 pts)** Write the general solution in the underdamped regime.

<Collapsible title="Corrigé and indicative barème">

**1. Classification (3 pts).**

$$
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ v \end{pmatrix}'
= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -\omega_0^2 & -\gamma \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ v \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\tau = -\gamma,
\quad
\Delta = \omega_0^2 > 0,
\quad
\tau^2 - 4\Delta = \gamma^2 - 4\omega_0^2 .
$$

With $\zeta = \gamma/(2\omega_0)$:

- $\zeta = 0$ ($\gamma = 0$): $\tau = 0$, eigenvalues
  $\pm i\omega_0$ — **linear center** (conservative oscillator);
- $0 < \zeta < 1$: complex eigenvalues with real part
  $-\gamma/2 < 0$ — **stable focus** (**underdamped** regime);
- $\zeta = 1$: double eigenvalue $-\omega_0$ — **critical regime**;
- $\zeta > 1$: two negative real eigenvalues — **stable node**
  (**overdamped** regime).

**2. Case $\gamma = 0$: Hamiltonian structure (3 pts).** Set

$$
H(x,v) = \frac12 v^2 + \frac12 \omega_0^2 x^2 .
$$

Then $\partial H/\partial v = v = x'$ and
$-\partial H/\partial x = -\omega_0^2 x = v'$: the system is
**Hamiltonian**, so $H$ is a first integral
($dH/dt = H_x x' + H_v v' = 0$). The trajectories are the level curves

$$
\frac12 v^2 + \frac12\omega_0^2 x^2 = C \ (C > 0) :
$$

**ellipses** centered at the origin (semiaxes $\sqrt{2C}/\omega_0$ in $x$
and $\sqrt{2C}$ in $v$), traversed periodically — the portrait of a center.

**3. Energy decrease (3 pts).** Along a trajectory, for
$\gamma > 0$:

$$
\frac{dE}{dt}
= v\,v' + \omega_0^2\,x\,x'
= v\bigl(-\omega_0^2 x - \gamma v\bigr) + \omega_0^2 x\,v
= -\gamma\,v^2 \ \le\ 0 .
$$

The mechanical energy **decreases** (strictly whenever $v \neq 0$): $E$ is
a Lyapunov function for the system, and all trajectories converge
to the origin — damping dissipates energy at the rate
$\gamma v^2$.

**4. No periodic orbit (3 pts).** Suppose there is a periodic solution
with period $T > 0$. Then $E(T) = E(0)$, and integrating the previous
result gives

$$
0 = E(T) - E(0) = -\gamma \int_0^T v(t)^2\,dt
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int_0^T v^2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v \equiv 0 .
$$

But $v \equiv 0$ gives $x' = 0$ ($x$ is constant), then
$0 = v' = -\omega_0^2 x$, so $x \equiv 0$: the only “periodic” orbit
is the equilibrium. **No nontrivial periodic orbit** exists for
$\gamma > 0$ — even the slightest dissipation destroys the center behavior.

**5. Underdamped solution (2 pts).** For $0 < \zeta < 1$, the
eigenvalues are $-\dfrac{\gamma}{2} \pm i\,\omega_d$, with damped angular frequency

$$
\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \frac{\gamma^2}{4}}
= \omega_0\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2},
$$

hence

$$
x(t) = e^{-\gamma t/2}\bigl(A\cos(\omega_d t) + B\sin(\omega_d t)\bigr),
\qquad A, B \in \mathbb R :
$$

oscillations with a decaying exponential envelope — the stable spiral
of the phase portrait, viewed as a function of time.

**What the examiner rewards.** Consistency among the five questions:
the classification (1) announces the regimes, the Hamiltonian structure (2)
explains the ellipses in the conservative case, and the calculation of $dE/dt$ (3) is
the tool that makes question (4) almost immediate — an *integral*
energy argument, much stronger than a drawing — while the solution (5)
ties everything back to the phase portrait. This is the
“Hamiltonian versus dissipation” through-line of the lesson.
