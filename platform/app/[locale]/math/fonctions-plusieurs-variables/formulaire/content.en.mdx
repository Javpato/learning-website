## Geometry and coordinates

<KeyResults title="Lines, planes, distance">

Parametric line: $\mathbf r(t) = \mathbf r_0 + t\,\mathbf u$.
Plane with normal vector $\mathbf n = (a,b,c)$ passing through $P_0$:
$a(x-x_0) + b(y-y_0) + c(z-z_0) = 0$.

Distance from a point $P = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$ to the plane $ax+by+cz+d = 0$:

$$
d(P, \Pi) = \frac{|a x_0 + b y_0 + c z_0 + d|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}} .
$$

Use: the normal vector can be read directly from the Cartesian equation — do not
confuse it with a direction vector.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Coordinate conversions">

<Terme id="coordonnees-polaires">Polar</Terme>: $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, $r \ge 0$.

<Terme id="coordonnees-cylindriques">Cylindrical</Terme>: $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, $z = z$.

<Terme id="coordonnees-spheriques">Spherical</Terme> (physics convention, $\theta$ = colatitude):

$$
x = r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,
\qquad
y = r\sin\theta\sin\varphi,
\qquad
z = r\cos\theta .
$$

Use: choose the system that matches the symmetry (atom → spherical, charged wire or
polymer → cylindrical, membrane → Cartesian). Watch the
quadrant when inverting with $\arctan(y/x)$.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Differential elements dS and dV">

$$
dS_{\text{polaire}} = r\,dr\,d\theta,
\qquad
dV_{\text{cyl}} = r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz,
\qquad
dV_{\text{sph}} = r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\varphi .
$$

Use: never forget the geometric factor $r$ or $r^2\sin\theta$ —
it is the classic error in every electromagnetism integral.

</KeyResults>

## Surfaces and level curves

<KeyResults title="Level-set method">

<Terme id="ligne-de-niveau">Level curve</Terme> with value $c$: $L_c = \{(x,y) : f(x,y) = c\}$ — in six
steps:

1. determine the domain;
2. exploit symmetries;
3. solve $f(x,y) = c$;
4. describe how the geometry changes with $c$;
5. inspect the <Terme id="fonction-partielle">partial functions</Terme>;
6. locate singular points.

Prototypes to recognize: plane $ax+by+c$, elliptic paraboloid
$x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2$, hyperbolic paraboloid $x^2/a^2 - y^2/b^2$ (<Terme id="point-selle">saddle</Terme>),
Gaussian hill $Ae^{-Q(x,y)}$, radial potential
$g\bigl(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\bigr)$.

Use: in chemistry, the <Terme id="extremum-local">minima</Terme> of a <Terme id="graphe">surface</Terme> $V(q_1,q_2)$ are <Terme id="stabilite">stable</Terme>
structures, and saddles are transition states.

</KeyResults>

## Limits and continuity

<KeyResults title="Path test and polar bound">

**Disprove** a <Terme id="limite">limit</Terme>: two paths ($y = mx$, $y = x^p$, a path adapted to the
denominator…) giving different values. Infinitely many paths
that agree prove **nothing**.

**Prove** a zero limit at the origin: use a polar bound

$$
|f(x,y)| \le C\,r^\alpha,
\qquad r = \sqrt{x^2+y^2},
\quad \alpha > 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} f = 0 .
$$

Use: $|x| \le r$, $|y| \le r$, and $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ are the building blocks of
all such bounds. Example: $x^2y/(x^2+y^2)$ satisfies $|f| \le r$.

</KeyResults>

## Differential, tangent plane, and gradient

<KeyResults title="Differential and tangent plane">

$$
df = f_x\,dx + f_y\,dy,
\qquad
z = f(a,b) + f_x(a,b)\,(x-a) + f_y(a,b)\,(y-b) .
$$

A sufficient condition for <Terme id="differentiabilite">differentiability</Terme>: **<Terme id="continuite">continuous</Terme>** <Terme id="derivee-partielle">partial derivatives</Terme>
in a neighborhood of the point (their mere existence is not enough). In the <Terme id="plan-tangent">tangent
plane</Terme>, the coefficients are **evaluated** at $(a,b)$ — never left as
functions of $x, y$.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Gradient, directional derivative, chain rule">

$$
\nabla f = (f_x,\ f_y),
\qquad
D_{\mathbf u} f = \nabla f\cdot\mathbf u
\quad (\|\mathbf u\| = 1),
\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}f\bigl(\mathbf r(t)\bigr) = \nabla f\cdot\mathbf r'(t) .
$$

$\nabla f$ is **normal** to regular <Terme id="ligne-de-niveau">level curves</Terme> and points in the
direction of steepest increase; $\|\nabla f\|$ is the maximum slope. For
composite maps: $D(f\circ g) = Df(g)\,Dg$ (product of the
<Terme id="jacobienne">Jacobians</Terme>). In thermodynamics: $dG = G_T\,dT + G_P\,dP$ — same
structure, different notation.

Use: always **normalize** the vector before taking a <Terme id="derivee-directionnelle">directional derivative</Terme>.

</KeyResults>

## Uncertainty propagation

<KeyResults title="The three propagation formulas">

Conservative bound (first order):

$$
|\Delta z| \lesssim \sum_i \left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}\right| \Delta x_i .
$$

**Independent** sources — combination in quadrature:

$$
\sigma_z^2 \approx \sum_i \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}\right)^{\!2}\sigma_i^2 .
$$

General case **with <Terme id="covariance">covariances</Terme>** ($\Sigma$ = covariance matrix):

$$
\sigma_z^2 \approx \nabla f^{\top}\,\Sigma\,\nabla f .
$$

Product of powers $z = C\,x^a y^b$: *relative* <Terme id="incertitude-type">uncertainties</Terme>
combine, with each exponent as a factor:

$$
\frac{dz}{z} = a\,\frac{dx}{x} + b\,\frac{dy}{y} .
$$

Use: first identify the dominant source (the largest term in the
sum) — that is the one to measure more accurately. Never apply the
power rule to a sum or difference.

</KeyResults>

## Critical points and extrema

<KeyResults title="Hessian test">

Interior <Terme id="point-critique">critical point</Terme>: $\nabla f = 0$. With
$D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2$ at the critical point:

- $D > 0$ and $f_{xx} > 0$: **<Terme id="extremum-local">local minimum</Terme>**;
- $D > 0$ and $f_{xx} < 0$: **local maximum**;
- $D < 0$: **<Terme id="point-selle">saddle point</Terme>**;
- $D = 0$: **inconclusive** test (study directly, often by
  factorization).

**Global** extrema on a compact domain: compare the interior (critical
points), boundary (curves), and corners. Extrema **subject to the constraint**
$g(x,y) = c$: $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$ (Lagrange multipliers).

Chemical use: all positive <Terme id="hessienne">Hessian</Terme> <Terme id="valeur-propre">eigenvalues</Terme> =
<Terme id="stabilite">stable</Terme> structure; exactly one negative eigenvalue = transition state of order 1.

</KeyResults>

## Differential systems and phase portraits

<KeyResults title="Nullclines and linearization">

<Terme id="systeme-differentiel">System</Terme> $x' = f(x,y)$, $y' = g(x,y)$: vector field $F = (f, g)$,
with <Terme id="point-d-equilibre">equilibria</Terme> at the points where $f = g = 0$.

<Terme id="isocline">Nullclines</Terme>: $f = 0$ (zero horizontal component) and $g = 0$
(zero vertical component); their intersections are the equilibria, and
sign analysis gives the direction of the arrows.

<Terme id="linearisation">Linearization</Terme> near an equilibrium $X_*$:

$$
u' \approx J_F(X_*)\,u,
\qquad
J_F = \begin{pmatrix} f_x & f_y \\ g_x & g_y \end{pmatrix}.
$$

Use: if no <Terme id="valeur-propre">eigenvalue</Terme> of $J_F(X_*)$ has zero real part, the
linear classification describes the local nonlinear behavior. In the
borderline case (linear center), linearization is not enough.

</KeyResults>

## Conservative and Hamiltonian systems

<KeyResults title="Hamiltonian versus gradient flow">

First integral: $H$ such that
$\dfrac{dH}{dt} = \nabla H\cdot F = 0$ — <Terme id="trajectoire">trajectories</Terme> lie on the
<Terme id="ligne-de-niveau">level curves</Terme> $H = C$.

Planar <Terme id="hamiltonien">Hamiltonian</Terme> system:

$$
x' = \frac{\partial H}{\partial y},
\qquad
y' = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{dH}{dt} = 0 .
$$

Mechanics of a particle:
$H(q,p) = \dfrac{p^2}{2m} + V(q)$, with $q' = p/m$ and $p' = -V'(q)$.

<Terme id="gradient">Gradient</Terme> flow $X' = -\nabla V$:

$$
\frac{dV}{dt} = -\|\nabla V\|^2 \le 0 .
$$

Use: Hamiltonian motion **follows** the level curves (energy
is conserved), while gradient flow **descends** them (energy is dissipated) — two
orthogonal dynamics on the same landscape.

</KeyResults>

## Linear systems and similarity

<KeyResults title="Solving X' = AX">

If $Av = \lambda v$, then $e^{\lambda t}v$ is a solution. With two independent
<Terme id="vecteur-propre">eigenvectors</Terme>:

$$
X(t) = c_1 e^{\lambda_1 t} v_1 + c_2 e^{\lambda_2 t} v_2 .
$$

Complex <Terme id="valeur-propre">eigenvalues</Terme> $\alpha \pm i\beta$: take the real and
imaginary parts of a complex solution. Jordan block:

$$
\exp\!\left(t\begin{pmatrix} \lambda & 1 \\ 0 & \lambda \end{pmatrix}\right)
= e^{\lambda t}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & t \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

Affine <Terme id="systeme-differentiel">system</Terme> $X' = AX + b$: find the <Terme id="point-d-equilibre">equilibrium</Terme> $X_*$, then set
$U = X - X_*$.

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Trace–determinant classification">

With $\tau = \operatorname{tr} A$, $\Delta = \det A$, and
$\mathcal D = \tau^2 - 4\Delta$:

| Conditions | Phase portrait |
| --- | --- |
| $\Delta < 0$ | <Terme id="point-selle">saddle point</Terme> (<Terme id="stabilite">unstable</Terme>) |
| $\Delta > 0$, $\mathcal D > 0$, $\tau < 0$ | stable <Terme id="noeud">node</Terme> |
| $\Delta > 0$, $\mathcal D > 0$, $\tau > 0$ | unstable node |
| $\Delta > 0$, $\mathcal D < 0$, $\tau < 0$ | stable <Terme id="foyer">focus (spiral)</Terme> |
| $\Delta > 0$, $\mathcal D < 0$, $\tau > 0$ | unstable focus (spiral) |
| $\tau = 0$, $\Delta > 0$, $\mathcal D < 0$ | linear center |
| repeated eigenvalue | examine diagonalizability |

Use: two invariants are enough to name the <Terme id="portrait-de-phase">portrait</Terme> — and the direction of
rotation is obtained by evaluating the field at a simple point, for example
$(1, 0)$.

</KeyResults>

## Harmonic oscillator and normal modes

<KeyResults title="Damping and regimes">

$m x'' + \gamma x' + k x = 0$ reduces, with $v = x'$, to

$$
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ v \end{pmatrix}'
= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -k/m & -\gamma/m \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ v \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
m\lambda^2 + \gamma\lambda + k = 0 .
$$

With $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$ and $\zeta = \dfrac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{mk}}$:

- $\zeta < 1$: **<Terme id="amortissement">underdamped</Terme>** — damped oscillations, damped angular frequency
  $\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}$;
- $\zeta = 1$: **critical** — fastest return without oscillation;
- $\zeta > 1$: **overdamped** — two decaying exponentials;
- $\gamma = 0$: <Terme id="systeme-conservatif">conservative</Terme>, $x = A\cos(\omega_0 t) + B\sin(\omega_0 t)$.

Forced <Terme id="oscillateur-harmonique">oscillator</Terme> (extension): steady-state amplitude

$$
A(\omega) = \frac{F_0}{\sqrt{(k - m\omega^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2}} .
$$

</KeyResults>

<KeyResults title="Normal modes and isotopic shift">

Coupled oscillators $Mq'' + Kq = 0$: the <Terme id="mode-normal">normal modes</Terme> satisfy

$$
(K - \omega^2 M)\,v = 0 .
$$

Two equal masses coupled by $\kappa$: symmetric mode $(1,1)$ with
$\omega^2 = k/m$, antisymmetric mode $(1,-1)$ with
$\omega^2 = (k + 2\kappa)/m$.

Diatomic oscillator with reduced mass $\mu$ — isotopic effect:

$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}},
\qquad
\frac{\omega'}{\omega} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\mu'}} .
$$

Use: the force constant $k$ does not change during isotopic
substitution — only $\mu$ changes, hence the shift of bands in
IR spectroscopy.

</KeyResults>
