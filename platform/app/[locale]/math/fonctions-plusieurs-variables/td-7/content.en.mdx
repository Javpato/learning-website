<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td7-01">

<Statement>Classify $x''+4x'+\omega_0^2x=0$ for $\omega_0=1,2,3$.</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>Compare $\gamma^2$ with $4mk$ or calculate the discriminant of $\lambda^2+4\lambda+\omega_0^2$.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>Overdamped for $\omega_0=1$, critically damped for $2$, underdamped for $3$.</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

The discriminant is $\Delta=16-4\omega_0^2$. It is positive for
$\omega_0=1$: two negative real roots. It is zero for
$\omega_0=2$: double root $-2$. It is negative for $\omega_0=3$:
$-2\pm i\sqrt5$, hence damped oscillations.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Calling the case in which one root is zero “critical.”
- Forgetting that the real part remains negative in the oscillatory regime.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td7-02">

<Statement>

For $mx''+\gamma x'+kx=0$, prove that
$E=\frac12mx'^2+\frac12kx^2$ satisfies $E'=-\gamma x'^2$.

</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>Differentiate $E$, factor by $x'$, then use the equation of motion.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>$E'=-\gamma x'^2\le0$ if $\gamma\ge0$.</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

$$
E'=mx'x''+kxx'=x'(mx''+kx).
$$

The equation gives $mx''+kx=-\gamma x'$, so
$E'=-\gamma x'^2$. For $\gamma=0$, energy is conserved; for
$\gamma>0$, it decreases except at times when the velocity is zero.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Writing $E'=-\gamma x'$ instead of $-\gamma x'^2$.
- Concluding that energy decreases strictly at every instant.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td7-03">

<Statement>

Two equal masses satisfy
$mx_1''=-(k+\kappa)x_1+\kappa x_2$ and
$mx_2''=\kappa x_1-(k+\kappa)x_2$.
Find the normal modes and their frequencies.

</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>Try the symmetric vector $(1,1)$ and the antisymmetric vector $(1,-1)$.</Hint>
<Hint>Solve $Kv=m\omega^2v$.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>

Symmetric mode $(1,1)$: $\omega_s^2=k/m$. Antisymmetric mode $(1,-1)$:
$\omega_a^2=(k+2\kappa)/m$.

</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

With $x(t)=v\cos\omega t$, the system becomes

$$
\begin{pmatrix}k+\kappa&-\kappa\\-\kappa&k+\kappa\end{pmatrix}v
=m\omega^2v.
$$

The matrix maps $(1,1)$ to $k(1,1)$: the coupling spring is not
stretched. It maps $(1,-1)$ to $(k+2\kappa)(1,-1)$: the spring contributes
twice. Every oscillation is a superposition of these two modes.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Assigning the highest frequency to the mode in which the masses move together.
- Forgetting the square in the relation between eigenvalue and frequency.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>

<ExerciseView id="math-fmv-td7-04">

<Statement>

A diatomic vibration is modeled by a spring with stiffness $k$ and the
reduced mass $\mu=m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)$. Give the frequency ratio after
isotopic substitution $\mu\to\mu'$.

</Statement>
<HintLadder>

<Hint>The natural frequency is $\omega=\sqrt{k/\mu}$.</Hint>
<Hint>Assume the chemical stiffness is unchanged to first order.</Hint>

</HintLadder>
<FinalAnswer>$\omega'/\omega=\sqrt{\mu/\mu'}$.</FinalAnswer>
<Solution>

Before substitution, $\omega=\sqrt{k/\mu}$; afterward,
$\omega'=\sqrt{k/\mu'}$. Taking the ratio eliminates $k$:

$$
\frac{\omega'}{\omega}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\mu'}}.
$$

A heavier isotope increases the reduced mass and therefore shifts the
vibrational band toward lower frequencies.

</Solution>
<CommonErrors>

- Using the total mass instead of the reduced mass.
- Reversing the ratio and predicting a higher frequency for the heavy isotope.

</CommonErrors>

</ExerciseView>
