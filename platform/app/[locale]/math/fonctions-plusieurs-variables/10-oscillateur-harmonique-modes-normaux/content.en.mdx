## Intuition

A mass at the end of a spring: the restoring force $-kx$ brings it back towards equilibrium, the inertia $m$ makes it exceed, and the friction $\gamma x'$ eats away the energy with each round trip. Three characters, three possible outcomes:

- low friction: the mass **oscillates** while slowly turning off (underdamped);
- enormous friction: it **crawls** towards equilibrium without ever oscillating (overdamped);
- between the two, a single setting: the fastest possible return without overshooting (**critical** damping).

A single dimensionless number, $\zeta$, says what world we are in.

<KeyResults title="Formulas to keep visible">

- Oscillator: $mx''+\gamma x'+kx=0$, $\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}$ and $\zeta=\gamma/(2\sqrt{mk})$.
- $\zeta<1$: damped oscillations; $\zeta=1$: critical damping; $\zeta>1$: return without oscillation.
- Diatomic bond: $\omega=\sqrt{k/\mu}$ with $\mu=m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)$.

</KeyResults>

## Formal definition

### Reduction to a first-order system

Let's set $v = x'$. The equation $mx'' + \gamma x' + kx = 0$ becomes

$$
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ v \end{pmatrix}'
=
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -k/m & -\gamma/m \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ v \end{pmatrix} :
$$

a linear system $X' = AX$ — all the arsenal from lesson 9 applies. Trace $\tau = -\gamma/m \le 0$, determinant $\Delta = k/m > 0$: the origin is always stable, focus or node depending on the discriminant.

### Characteristic equation and regimes

<Rappel title="Solve a linear ODE with constant coefficients">

The $x=e^{\lambda t}$ test transforms the ODE into a polynomial in $\lambda$. Two real roots give exponentials; a pair $a\pm ib$ gives $e^{at}\cos bt$ and $e^{at}\sin bt$.

</Rappel>

Searching for $x = e^{\lambda t}$ gives **characteristic equation**

$$
m\lambda^2 + \gamma\lambda + k = 0,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{-\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - 4mk}}{2m}.
$$

Let us introduce the two physical parameters:

$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
\quad\text{(pulsation propre)},
\qquad
\zeta = \frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{mk}}
\quad\text{(taux d'amortissement)}.
$$

<KeyResults title="The three regimes (+ the conservative case)">

The sign of $\gamma^2 - 4mk$, that is to say the position of $\zeta$ relative to $1$:

- $\zeta < 1$ — **underdamped**: $\lambda = -\zeta\omega_0 \pm i\omega_d$ with $\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$; oscillations in an envelope $e^{-\zeta\omega_0 t}$;
- $\zeta = 1$ — **critical**: double root $\lambda = -\omega_0$, solution $(A + Bt)\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$ (the $t$ factor of Jordan block, lesson 9); fastest return without oscillation;
- $\zeta > 1$ — **overdamped**: two negative real roots, sum of two decreasing exponentials, no oscillation;
- $\gamma = 0$ — **conservative**: $\lambda = \pm i\omega_0$, linear center, perpetual oscillation

$$
x(t) = A\cos(\omega_0 t) + B\sin(\omega_0 t)
= C\cos(\omega_0 t - \varphi),
$$

with $C = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$; the energy $E = \tfrac12 mx'^2 + \tfrac12 kx^2$ is conserved (this is Hamiltonian system from lesson 8 with $V = \tfrac12 kq^2$).

</KeyResults>

</Collapsible>

## Worked example

Let's solve $x'' + 2x' + 5x = 0$ with $x(0) = 1$, $x'(0) = 0$ (here $m = 1$, $\gamma = 2$, $k = 5$).

**Step 1 — settings.** $\omega_0 = \sqrt5$ and $\zeta = \dfrac{2}{2\sqrt5} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt5} \approx 0.447 < 1$: **underdamped** regime.

**Step 2 — characteristic equation.** $\lambda^2 + 2\lambda + 5 = 0$ gives $\lambda = -1 \pm 2i$: decay $e^{-t}$, damped angular frequency $\omega_d = 2$. (Check: $\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta^2} = \sqrt5\cdot\sqrt{4/5} = 2$. ✔)

**Step 3 — general solution.**

$$
x(t) = e^{-t}\bigl(A\cos 2t + B\sin 2t\bigr).
$$

**Step 4 — initial conditions.** $x(0) = A = 1$. Afterwards

$$
x'(t) = e^{-t}\bigl[(-A + 2B)\cos 2t + (-B - 2A)\sin 2t\bigr],
$$

therefore $x'(0) = -A + 2B = 0$, hence $B = \tfrac12$:

$$
x(t) = e^{-t}\left(\cos 2t + \tfrac12\sin 2t\right).
$$

**Step 5 — physical reading.** Amplitude divided by $e \approx 2.72$ every time unit, about $\omega_d/(2\pi/1) \approx 0.32$ oscillations per unit time; in the $(x, v)$ phase plane, a converging spiral — the stable focus predicted by $\tau = -2$, $\Delta = 5$, $\mathcal D = 4 - 20 < 0$.

<Collapsible title="Demonstration — the resonance amplitude A(ω) by the complex ansatz">

Let's look for the stationary response of $mx'' + \gamma x' + kx = F_0\cos\omega t$. Let's move on to complex: we solve

$$
m z'' + \gamma z' + k z = F_0 e^{i\omega t},
$$

and the physical solution will be $x = \operatorname{Re} z$. The ansatz $z = \hat z\,e^{i\omega t}$ gives, after division by $e^{i\omega t}$:

$$
\bigl(-m\omega^2 + i\gamma\omega + k\bigr)\,\hat z = F_0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\hat z = \frac{F_0}{(k - m\omega^2) + i\gamma\omega}.
$$

The real amplitude is the modulus:

$$
A(\omega) = |\hat z|
= \frac{F_0}{\sqrt{(k - m\omega^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2}},
$$

and the denominator argument gives the phase delay $\tan\varphi = \dfrac{\gamma\omega}{k - m\omega^2}$.

**Analysis of the peak.** The denominator is minimal in $\omega_r = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$ (which exists if $\zeta < 1/\sqrt2$): the resonance peak is *slightly below* $\omega_0$, the smaller $\gamma$ is, the taller and narrower it becomes. For $\gamma \to 0$, $A(\omega_0) \to \infty$: the resonance catastrophe. $\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

## Forced oscillator and resonance <ProvenanceBadge provenance="extension" />

With periodic forcing,

$$
mx'' + \gamma x' + kx = F_0\cos\omega t,
$$

General solution = (damped transient, which dies in $e^{-\zeta\omega_0 t}$)
+ (**steady state** at the imposed frequency $\omega$), amplitude

$$
A(\omega) = \frac{F_0}{\sqrt{(k - m\omega^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2}}
$$

(foldable demonstration above). As $\omega$ sweeps through the frequencies, $A(\omega)$ peaks near $\omega_0$: this is **resonance**. This is exactly the principle of infrared spectroscopy: the electromagnetic field forces the bonds, and absorption is maximum at the natural frequencies of the vibration modes.

## Coupled oscillators and normal modes <ProvenanceBadge provenance="extension" />

For several coupled masses, the equation becomes matrix:

$$
M\,q'' + K\,q = 0,
$$

with $M$ the mass matrix and $K$ that of the stiffnesses. We are looking for solutions where *everything vibrates in phase at the same frequency* — the **normal modes** $q(t) = \cos(\omega t)\,v$:

$$
(K - \omega^2 M)\,v = 0 :
$$

a widespread problem with eigenvalues. The $\omega^2$ are the roots of $\det(K - \omega^2M) = 0$, and the $v$ vectors give the *shape* of the movement. All vibration is a superposition of these modes.

**Example.** Two masses $m$ connected by three springs (wall–$m$–$m$–wall, stiffnesses $k$, $\kappa$, $k$): the **symmetric** mode $(1,1)$ (the central spring does not stretch) vibrates at $\omega^2 = k/m$, **anti-symmetric** mode $(1,-1)$ (the central spring stretches twice as much) to $\omega^2 = (k + 2\kappa)/m$. TD 7, exercise 03, works this out in full.

## Link with chemistry

A diatomic bond is an oscillator of **reduced** mass $\mu = \dfrac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$ and force constant $k$ (the curvature of the potential to the minimum — the Hessian of lesson 6):

$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}} .
$$

Substituting an isotope does **not** change $k$ (same electron cloud, same potential) but changes $\mu$: the frequencies move as

$$
\frac{\omega'}{\omega} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\mu'}} .
$$

For H → D in an X–H bond, $\mu$ almost double, hence $\omega' \approx \omega/\sqrt2$ : the O–H elongation band towards $3600\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ falls towards $2600\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ in O–D. This **isotopic shift** is such a reliable experimental signature that it is used to assign IR bands and elucidate reaction mechanisms (isotope kinetic effect). For a polyatomic molecule, the normal modes of $(K - \omega^2M)v = 0$ *are* the vibration modes observed in IR and Raman.

## Common pitfalls

<Pitfall>

**Confuse $\omega_0$ with damped angular frequency $\omega_d$.** In underdamped regime, the system oscillates at $\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} < \omega_0$, not at $\omega_0$. And the forced resonance peak is still elsewhere: $\omega_r = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$. Three distinct frequencies that only coincide when $\gamma = 0$.

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**Write two exponentials at critical damping.** When $\zeta = 1$, the root is double: the solution is $(A + Bt)e^{-\omega_0 t}$, not $Ae^{\lambda_1 t} + Be^{\lambda_2 t}$. This is the factor $t$ from the Jordan block (lesson 9) — forgetting it loses a constant of integration.

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**Use the mass of an atom instead of reduced mass.** For a diatomic bond, $\mu = m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)$ goes into $\omega = \sqrt{k/\mu}$. For HCl, $\mu \approx 0.97$ u — close to $m_\mathrm H$ but not equal; for two equal masses, $\mu = m/2$, not $m$.

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**Believe that the isotope changes the force constant.** $k$ is the curvature of the electronic potential, identical for all isotopes (Born–Oppenheimer approximation). Only $\mu$ changes — that's what makes the $\omega'/\omega = \sqrt{\mu/\mu'}$ ratio so clean and predictive.

</Pitfall>

<MissionSolved>

Isotopic substitution leaves $k$ almost unchanged but gives $\mu'\approx2\mu$. Thus $\omega'/\omega=\sqrt{\mu/\mu'}\approx1/\sqrt2$, and $3600/\sqrt2\approx2550\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, close to the O–D band observed around $2600\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. The quadratic bottom of the well therefore directly converts an isotopic mass into an IR signature.

## Lesson summary

<KeyResults title="The essentials">

- Reduction: $v = x'$ transforms $mx'' + \gamma x' + kx = 0$ into $X' = AX$ with $A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -k/m & -\gamma/m \end{pmatrix}$.
- characteristic equation $m\lambda^2 + \gamma\lambda + k = 0$ ; parameters $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$, $\zeta = \gamma/(2\sqrt{mk})$.
- Schemes: $\zeta < 1$ underdamped ($\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}$), $\zeta = 1$ critical ($(A+Bt)e^{-\omega_0t}$), $\zeta > 1$ overdamped; $\gamma = 0$: $x = A\cos\omega_0t + B\sin\omega_0t$, conserved energy.
- Forced (extension): $A(\omega) = F_0/\sqrt{(k-m\omega^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2}$, resonance near $\omega_0$ for $\gamma$ small.
- Coupled (extension): normal modes solutions of $(K - \omega^2M)v = 0$; all vibration is a superposition of modes.
- Chemistry: $\omega = \sqrt{k/\mu}$ with reduced mass; $\omega'/\omega = \sqrt{\mu/\mu'}$ — the isotopic shift of IR spectroscopy.

</KeyResults>
