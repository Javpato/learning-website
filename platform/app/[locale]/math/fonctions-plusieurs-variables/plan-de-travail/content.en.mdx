# Study plan — 12 weeks (mathematics + physics)

This plan is **purely indicative** — nothing in it is mandatory, there are no boxes to
check, and no order is imposed. All site content remains open at
all times: you can explore in any order, skip a week,
spread one over two weeks, or select only what interests you. This
schedule exists only for those who like having a through-line: it synchronizes the
mathematics module with the physics track (electromagnetism), so that each
mathematical tool arrives just before physics needs it.

## How to use this plan

One method that works well for each lesson — adapt it freely:

1. **Diagnosis** — attempt the opening questions without notes, simply to
   see where you are starting from (nothing is at stake; nobody is watching);
2. **Theory** — study the compact lesson section;
3. **Reconstruction** — try to reproduce the main proof from
   memory: this is the step that truly anchors it;
4. **Core exercise** — one core exercise from the associated TD (tutorial sheet);
5. **Transfer** — a problem in a chemical context, to give it meaning;
6. **Return after 48 h** — come back two days later to a mixed problem:
   memory loves being prompted just before it forgets;
7. **Difficult TD** — save the starred exercises until the basic mechanisms
   have become automatic. Not before — and without guilt.

A realistic weekly pace (adjust it to your week):

- **Mathematics**: two 90-minute blocks + one 2-hour TD block;
- **Physics**: one 90-minute theory block + one 2-hour problem
  block;
- **Cumulative review**: 30 to 45 minutes over the weekend, drawing from the
  [formula sheet](../formulaire) and previous weeks.

And if a week spills over: resuming where you left off is exactly
the right strategy. The plan is a guide, never a judge.
