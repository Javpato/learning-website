# Open and closed balls

<Accroche>« The ball $B(x, r)$ is everything within distance $< r$ of $x$ — and its shape depends on the chosen norm. »</Accroche>

## Intuition

A ball is a **neighbourhood**: "everything close enough to $x$". The **open / closed** nuance is one
of threshold: strictly closer than $r$ (the boundary is excluded), or at most $r$ (the boundary is
included). And since "close" is measured with a norm, the **shape** of the ball changes with it: a
disk for $\lVert\cdot\rVert_2$, a diamond for $\lVert\cdot\rVert_1$, a square for
$\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$. The idea — a neighbourhood around $x$ — stays the same.

## Formal definition

Let $(E, \lVert\cdot\rVert)$ be a normed vector space, $x \in E$ and $r > 0$.

$$B(x, r) = \{\, y \in E : \lVert y - x \rVert < r \,\} \quad\text{(open ball)},$$
$$\overline{B}(x, r) = \{\, y \in E : \lVert y - x \rVert \le r \,\} \quad\text{(closed ball)},$$
$$S(x, r) = \{\, y \in E : \lVert y - x \rVert = r \,\} \quad\text{(sphere)}.$$

In $\mathbb{R}^2$, the closed unit ball is the **disk** $\{x^2+y^2\le1\}$ for $\lVert\cdot\rVert_2$,
the **diamond** $\{|x|+|y|\le1\}$ for $\lVert\cdot\rVert_1$, and the **square**
$\{\max(|x|,|y|)\le1\}$ for $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$.

## Worked example

For $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$: $B\bigl((0,0),1\bigr) = (-1,1)\times(-1,1)$, the
**open** square. The point $(0.9,\,-0.5)$ satisfies $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty = 0.9 < 1$: it is
**inside** the open ball. The point $(1,\,0.3)$ has $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty = 1$: it is on the
**sphere**, hence in $\overline{B}$ but not in $B$.

## Proof

<Collapsible title="An open ball is open (the key to everything)" open={true}>

Let us show that every point of $B(x, r)$ **also** has a small ball entirely contained in $B(x, r)$.
Let $y \in B(x, r)$, and set $\rho = r - \lVert y - x\rVert > 0$. Then for every $z \in B(y, \rho)$,

$$\lVert z - x \rVert \le \lVert z - y \rVert + \lVert y - x \rVert < \rho + \lVert y - x\rVert = r,$$

so $z \in B(x, r)$. Thus $B(y, \rho) \subseteq B(x, r)$. Every point of the ball has room around it:
this is **exactly** the definition of an open set (next page). $\qquad\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="A closed ball is closed">

Its complement $\{\,y : \lVert y - x\rVert > r\,\}$ is open: if $\lVert y - x\rVert > r$, set
$\rho = \lVert y - x\rVert - r > 0$; for $z \in B(y,\rho)$,
$\lVert z - x\rVert \ge \lVert y - x\rVert - \lVert z - y\rVert > r$, so $z$ is still outside
$\overline B(x,r)$. The complement is open, hence $\overline B(x,r)$ is closed. $\qquad\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="Equivalent norms ⟹ comparable balls">

If $a\,\lVert\cdot\rVert' \le \lVert\cdot\rVert \le b\,\lVert\cdot\rVert'$, then the ball of one is
trapped between two balls of the other: $B'\bigl(x, r/b\bigr) \subseteq B(x, r) \subseteq B'\bigl(x, r/a\bigr)$.
This is why, in finite dimension (where all norms are equivalent), **the open sets do not depend on
the chosen norm**: the topology is the same.

</Collapsible>

## Common pitfalls

<Pitfall>

**Open ≠ closed, and "neither" exists.** $B(x,r)$ (strict) is open, $\overline B(x,r)$ (non-strict)
is closed. But a set like $\{\,y : r_1 \le \lVert y-x\rVert < r_2\,\}$ (a half-open annulus) is
**neither** open **nor** closed. "Closed" is not "not open".

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**A ball need not be round.** For $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$ it is a square, for $\lVert\cdot\rVert_1$
a diamond. The "disk" intuition is specific to the Euclidean norm.

</Pitfall>

## Mini-exercises

<Collapsible title="1. Show that a ball (for any norm) is convex.">

Let $y, z \in B(x, r)$ and $t \in [0,1]$. By homogeneity and the triangle inequality,
$\lVert (1-t)y + t z - x\rVert \le (1-t)\lVert y-x\rVert + t\lVert z-x\rVert < (1-t)r + tr = r$.
So the segment $[y,z]$ stays in the ball: it is convex.

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="2. Describe B∞((0,0), 1) and B₁((0,0), 1) in the plane.">

$B_\infty = (-1,1)^2$, the open square with axis-parallel sides. $B_1 = \{|x|+|y|<1\}$, the open
diamond with vertices $(\pm1,0)$ and $(0,\pm1)$. One always has $B_1 \subseteq B_2 \subseteq B_\infty$
(diamond inside disk inside square).

</Collapsible>
