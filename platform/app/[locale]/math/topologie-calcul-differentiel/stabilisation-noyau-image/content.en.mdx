# Stabilisation of the kernel and the image

<Accroche>« Iterate $f$: the kernel swells, the image shrinks — and the two freeze *at exactly the same instant*. »</Accroche>

Let $E$ be a **finite-dimensional** vector space of dimension $n$ and $f \in \mathcal{L}(E)$ an
endomorphism. We study the equivalence

$$\ker f = \ker f^2 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \operatorname{im} f = \operatorname{im} f^2.$$

## Intuition

Applying $f$ crushes the space a little: some directions go to $0$ (the **kernel**), and what
survives forms the **image**. Doing it again with $f^2$ crushes more. Two natural chains appear,
one increasing, one decreasing:

$$\{0\} \subseteq \ker f \subseteq \ker f^2 \subseteq \cdots, \qquad E \supseteq \operatorname{im} f \supseteq \operatorname{im} f^2 \supseteq \cdots.$$

The kernel can only **grow**, the image can only **shrink**. The question is: when does it stop?
The remarkable answer is that the two phenomena are *the same phenomenon seen from two sides*:
as soon as the kernel stops growing by one step, the image stops shrinking — and conversely. It
is **dimension** (the rank–nullity theorem) that makes this inevitable.

## Formal definition

The two basic inclusions are free and hold in any dimension:

$$\ker f \subseteq \ker f^2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \operatorname{im} f^2 \subseteq \operatorname{im} f.$$

In finite dimension, the **rank–nullity theorem** ties the two chains together:

$$\dim \ker f^k + \dim \operatorname{im} f^k = n \quad \text{for every } k \ge 0.$$

**Theorem.** In finite dimension, $\ \ker f = \ker f^2 \iff \operatorname{im} f = \operatorname{im} f^2$.
The chains are then said to be **stabilised at index $1$**.

## Worked example

On $\mathbb{R}^2$ take the **nilpotent** matrix $N = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.
Then $N^2 = 0$, and so

$$\ker N = \operatorname{span}(e_1) \subsetneq \ker N^2 = \mathbb{R}^2, \qquad \operatorname{im} N = \operatorname{span}(e_1) \supsetneq \operatorname{im} N^2 = \{0\}.$$

**Both** equalities fail *at once*: the kernel grew ($1 \to 2$) exactly when the image shrank
($1 \to 0$). At the opposite end, for the **projection**
$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ we have $P^2 = P$, so $\ker P = \ker P^2$ and
$\operatorname{im} P = \operatorname{im} P^2$: everything is stabilised. Neither equality is ever true
without the other.

## Proof

<Collapsible title="Step 0 — the inclusions and the rank–nullity theorem" open={true}>

**Inclusions.** If $x \in \ker f$ then $f(x) = 0$, so $f^2(x) = f\bigl(f(x)\bigr) = f(0) = 0$,
hence $x \in \ker f^2$: thus $\ker f \subseteq \ker f^2$. Likewise, if $y \in \operatorname{im} f^2$,
write $y = f^2(x) = f\bigl(f(x)\bigr) \in \operatorname{im} f$, hence $\operatorname{im} f^2 \subseteq \operatorname{im} f$.

**Rank.** The rank–nullity theorem applied to $f^k$ gives
$\dim \ker f^k + \dim \operatorname{im} f^k = n$.

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="Step 1 — proof via dimensions (the heart)">

An inclusion of subspaces **of the same finite dimension is an equality**. With step 0,

$$\ker f = \ker f^2 \iff \dim \ker f = \dim \ker f^2,$$
$$\operatorname{im} f = \operatorname{im} f^2 \iff \dim \operatorname{im} f = \dim \operatorname{im} f^2.$$

Now rank–nullity gives $\dim \ker f^k = n - \dim \operatorname{im} f^k$. Therefore

$$\dim \ker f = \dim \ker f^2 \iff n - \dim \operatorname{im} f = n - \dim \operatorname{im} f^2 \iff \dim \operatorname{im} f = \dim \operatorname{im} f^2.$$

Chaining the three equivalences:
$\ker f = \ker f^2 \iff \operatorname{im} f = \operatorname{im} f^2$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="Step 2 — the geometric meaning: ker f ∩ im f = {0}">

We can say *why*, without counting dimensions. Let us prove
$\ker f = \ker f^2 \iff \ker f \cap \operatorname{im} f = \{0\}$.

- **($\Rightarrow$)** Let $y \in \ker f \cap \operatorname{im} f$. Write $y = f(x)$. Since
  $y \in \ker f$, $f^2(x) = f(y) = 0$, so $x \in \ker f^2 = \ker f$, hence $y = f(x) = 0$.
- **($\Leftarrow$)** Let $x \in \ker f^2$. Then $f(x) \in \ker f \cap \operatorname{im} f = \{0\}$,
  so $f(x) = 0$, i.e. $x \in \ker f$. Thus $\ker f^2 \subseteq \ker f$, hence equality.

Dually, $\operatorname{im} f = \operatorname{im} f^2 \iff E = \ker f + \operatorname{im} f$. In finite
dimension, $\dim(\ker f) + \dim(\operatorname{im} f) = n$ (rank–nullity), so "direct sum"
($\ker f \cap \operatorname{im} f = \{0\}$) and "sum equals $E$" ($\ker f + \operatorname{im} f = E$)
are equivalent — which **re-proves** the theorem and reveals its content: at stabilisation,

$$E = \ker f \oplus \operatorname{im} f.$$

This is the **Fitting decomposition** at index $1$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

## Common pitfalls

<Pitfall>

**The finite-dimension hypothesis is essential.** On the space of sequences, the right shift
$R(x_1, x_2, \dots) = (0, x_1, x_2, \dots)$ is **injective**, so

$$\ker R = \ker R^2 = \{0\} \quad\text{(equality of the kernels!)},$$

but $\operatorname{im} R = \{x : x_1 = 0\}$ whereas $\operatorname{im} R^2 = \{x : x_1 = x_2 = 0\}$, so
$\operatorname{im} R^2 \subsetneq \operatorname{im} R$. The equivalence **breaks down**: without
rank–nullity (which no longer makes sense here), the kernel can freeze while the image keeps
shrinking.

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**"$\ker f = \ker f^2$" does not say "$f$ injective".** The projection
$P = \mathrm{diag}(1,0)$ satisfies $\ker P = \ker P^2 = \operatorname{span}(e_2) \neq \{0\}$.
The equality means the kernel chain is *stationary from index $1$ on*, not that it is trivial.

</Pitfall>

## Mini-exercises

<Collapsible title="1. Show that both equalities are equivalent to 'rank f = rank f²'.">

By the inclusions and equality of dimensions: $\ker f = \ker f^2 \iff \dim \ker f = \dim \ker f^2$,
and $\dim \ker f^k = n - \operatorname{rank} f^k$. So equality of the kernels is equivalent to
$\operatorname{rank} f = \operatorname{rank} f^2$, and likewise for the images. This is exactly the
test computed by the widget.

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="2. Deduce the decomposition E = ker f ⊕ im f.">

If $\ker f = \ker f^2$, step 2 gives $\ker f \cap \operatorname{im} f = \{0\}$; rank–nullity supplies
$\dim \ker f + \dim \operatorname{im} f = n$. A direct sum of subspaces whose dimensions add up to $n$
fills $E$: therefore $E = \ker f \oplus \operatorname{im} f$.

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="3. Generalise: why do the chains stabilise at the SAME index?">

The sequences $\dim \ker f^k$ (increasing) and $\dim \operatorname{im} f^k$ (decreasing) take values
in $\{0, \dots, n\}$, hence are eventually constant. The same proof, with $f^k$ and $f^{k+1}$ in
place of $f$ and $f^2$, shows $\ker f^k = \ker f^{k+1} \iff \operatorname{im} f^k = \operatorname{im} f^{k+1}$.
Both chains therefore freeze at the same index $p$ (the **index** of $f$), and one obtains the
general Fitting decomposition $E = \ker f^p \oplus \operatorname{im} f^p$.

</Collapsible>
