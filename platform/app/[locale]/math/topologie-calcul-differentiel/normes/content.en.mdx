# Norms

<Accroche>« A norm is a rule for measuring the size of a vector — and there are several, which give different geometries. »</Accroche>

## Intuition

Measuring the "length" of a vector seems obvious in the plane: take $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. But that is
only one choice among many. One can also measure by summing the displacements ($|x| + |y|$, the
"city-block" distance), or by keeping only the largest coordinate ($\max(|x|,|y|)$). Each choice
deforms the **unit ball**: a diamond, a disk, a square. And on a space of **functions**, "the size
of $f$" can mean its area $\int |f|$, its energy $\bigl(\int f^2\bigr)^{1/2}$, or its maximum
$\sup |f|$ — three genuinely different rulers.

## Formal definition

Let $E$ be a real vector space. A **norm** on $E$ is a map $N \colon E \to \mathbb{R}_+$ such that,
for all $x, y \in E$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\textbf{(separation)}\quad N(x) = 0 \iff x = 0,$$
$$\textbf{(homogeneity)}\quad N(\lambda x) = |\lambda|\, N(x),$$
$$\textbf{(triangle inequality)}\quad N(x + y) \le N(x) + N(y).$$

One often writes $N(x) = \lVert x \rVert$. On $\mathbb{R}^n$, the three usual norms are

$$\lVert x \rVert_1 = \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|, \qquad \lVert x \rVert_2 = \Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\Bigr)^{1/2}, \qquad \lVert x \rVert_\infty = \max_{1 \le i \le n} |x_i|.$$

On the space $C([0,1])$ of continuous functions, one has the **integral** norms

$$\lVert f \rVert_1 = \int_0^1 |f(t)|\,dt, \qquad \lVert f \rVert_2 = \Bigl(\int_0^1 f(t)^2\,dt\Bigr)^{1/2}, \qquad \lVert f \rVert_\infty = \sup_{t \in [0,1]} |f(t)|.$$

## Worked example

Let us check that $N_1(f) = \int_0^1 |f|$ is a norm on $C([0,1])$. **Homogeneity** and the
**triangle inequality** are immediate: $\int |\lambda f| = |\lambda|\int|f|$ and
$\int|f+g| \le \int(|f|+|g|) = \int|f| + \int|g|$ by monotonicity of the integral. The delicate
point is **separation**: if $\int_0^1 |f| = 0$ with $f$ **continuous**, then $f = 0$. Indeed, if
$f(t_0) \neq 0$, continuity gives $|f| \ge |f(t_0)|/2 > 0$ on a small interval, so $\int |f| > 0$ —
a contradiction. *Continuity is essential here.*

For $f(t) = t$: $\ \lVert f \rVert_1 = \int_0^1 t\,dt = \tfrac12$, $\ \lVert f \rVert_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$, $\ \lVert f \rVert_\infty = 1$.

## Proof

<Collapsible title="Comparing the norms on ℝⁿ (they are equivalent)" open={true}>

For every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$,

$$\lVert x \rVert_\infty \le \lVert x \rVert_2 \le \sqrt{n}\,\lVert x \rVert_\infty.$$

*Left:* $\max_i |x_i|^2 \le \sum_i x_i^2$, so $\lVert x\rVert_\infty \le \lVert x\rVert_2$.
*Right:* $\sum_i x_i^2 \le n \max_i x_i^2$, so $\lVert x\rVert_2 \le \sqrt n\,\lVert x\rVert_\infty$.
Two norms $N, N'$ are **equivalent** if there exist $a, b > 0$ with $a\,N \le N' \le b\,N$. We have
just shown it for $\lVert\cdot\rVert_2$ and $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$.

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="Theorem — in finite dimension, all norms are equivalent">

Let $E$ be finite-dimensional and $N$ any norm. Fixing a basis, compare $N$ with
$\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$. The unit sphere $S = \{x : \lVert x\rVert_\infty = 1\}$ is **closed and
bounded**, hence **compact** ($\dim < \infty$). The map $N$ is continuous (by the triangle
inequality) and does not vanish on $S$; by compactness it attains there a minimum $a > 0$ and a
maximum $b$. By homogeneity, $a\,\lVert x\rVert_\infty \le N(x) \le b\,\lVert x\rVert_\infty$ for all
$x$. So $N \sim \lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$, and by transitivity **all** norms are equivalent. $\qquad\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="In infinite dimension, this is FALSE">

On $C([0,1])$, take the "spikes" $f_n(t) = \max(0,\,1 - n\,t)$ (triangle of height $1$ and base
$1/n$). Then $\lVert f_n \rVert_\infty = 1$ for all $n$, while
$\lVert f_n \rVert_1 = \int_0^{1/n}(1 - nt)\,dt = \tfrac{1}{2n} \to 0$. The ratio
$\lVert f_n\rVert_\infty / \lVert f_n\rVert_1 = 2n \to \infty$: no constant $b$ can satisfy
$\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty \le b\,\lVert\cdot\rVert_1$. The two norms are **not equivalent**. This is
exactly the "narrow spike" preset of the widget. $\qquad\blacksquare$

</Collapsible>

## Common pitfalls

<Pitfall>

**Separation of $\int|f|$ relies on continuity.** On the larger space of merely integrable
functions, $f \mapsto \int|f|$ is only a **seminorm**: a function that is zero everywhere except at
one point has zero integral without being the zero function. That is why one works on $C([0,1])$
(or, later, on classes of functions equal almost everywhere).

</Pitfall>

<Pitfall>

**For $p < 1$, $\lVert\cdot\rVert_p$ is not a norm.** The triangle inequality fails: on
$\mathbb{R}^2$ with $p = \tfrac12$, $\lVert(1,0)\rVert_{1/2} + \lVert(0,1)\rVert_{1/2} = 1 + 1 = 2$
but $\lVert(1,1)\rVert_{1/2} = (1+1)^{2} = 4 > 2$. The "ball" becomes concave (star-shaped).

</Pitfall>

## Mini-exercises

<Collapsible title="1. Bound ‖x‖₁ by ‖x‖₂ on ℝⁿ.">

Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\lVert x\rVert_1 = \sum |x_i|\cdot 1 \le \sqrt{n}\,\lVert x\rVert_2$. And
$\lVert x\rVert_2^2 = \sum x_i^2 \le \bigl(\sum|x_i|\bigr)^2 = \lVert x\rVert_1^2$, i.e.
$\lVert x\rVert_2 \le \lVert x\rVert_1$. So $\lVert x\rVert_2 \le \lVert x\rVert_1 \le \sqrt n\,\lVert x\rVert_2$.

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="2. A sequence of functions with sup-norm 1 but L¹-norm tending to 0.">

The triangles $f_n(t) = \max(0, 1 - n t)$ work: $\lVert f_n\rVert_\infty = 1$ and
$\lVert f_n\rVert_1 = \tfrac{1}{2n} \to 0$. One may also take "bumps" $g_n$ of height $1$ and width
$1/n$ centred at $1/2$.

</Collapsible>

<Collapsible title="3. Why does the equivalence proof fail in infinite dimension?">

It uses the **compactness** of the unit sphere. In infinite dimension, the unit sphere is **never**
compact (Riesz's theorem): one loses the argument that the minimum is attained, and indeed the
equivalence is false, as the spikes show.

</Collapsible>
